AI agents visit your site daily. What policy do AI agents find there?

AI agents read your site before your customers do: crawling it for training, fetching it to answer questions, and increasingly transacting on it. Every one of those visits happens under rules you never published. robots.txt, written for 1994 crawlers, can say 'stay out'. It cannot say 'agents may read prices, may not scrape reviews, and may transact up to this limit with attribution'.

Why publish rules for agents instead of just blocking them?

Because blocking is a wall and a policy is a door with terms. Some agents are customers in waiting, some are search infrastructure you want, and only some are pure extraction. A published policy separates them in writing: it invites the welcome, conditions the tolerated and strengthens your position against the rest, in a form agents and courts can both read.

Publish your agent policy: the working order

  1. Decide the three buckets: which agents may read, which may transact, which are refused, and the limits on each. This is a business decision, not a technical one.
  2. Write it in machine form: an agent-policy.json at /.well-known/ naming agents, actions, limits, terms and a contact. AgentGate's generator drafts it from your answers.
  3. Keep robots.txt aligned: the old file and the new file should never contradict. Agents read both; disputes will too.
  4. Link it where agents look: reference the policy from your llms.txt and your site footer, so discovery does not depend on the well-known path alone.
  5. Review on the traffic: watch which agents actually visit (your logs know) and revise the policy as the ecosystem moves. Standards are young; early publishers shape them.

Frequently asked questions

What is an agent-access policy?

An agent-access policy is a machine-readable declaration of how AI agents may use your site: which agents are welcome, what actions they may take, what limits and terms apply, and who to contact. AgentGate publishes it as agent-policy.json in the /.well-known/ directory, where agents and tools are learning to look for it.

What is AgentGate?

AgentGate is Ingenious AI's agent-access policy standard and tooling. It generates and hosts the policy file at /.well-known/agent-policy.json, declaring named agents, permitted actions, limits and terms. Where robots.txt gave crawlers one bit of information, refuse or allow, AgentGate gives agents the actual rules of engagement for your site.

How is this different from robots.txt?

robots.txt, written in 1994, answers one question for crawlers: may you fetch this path. An agent policy answers the agent-era questions: which agents, doing what, how much, under what terms, and who to contact. The files complement each other; a serious site keeps both consistent, because agents and disputes read both.

What about the agent.json standard?

agent.json is a rival proposal from the Agent Web Protocol project, currently at draft v0.2, aiming at the same /.well-known/ slot. Standards competition is normal at this stage. AgentGate's bet is policy depth: named agents, actions, limits and terms. Publishing a policy now matters more than which draft ultimately wins, because terms you publish are yours under any standard.

Does a policy file stop bad agents?

No, and it does not claim to. A policy governs honest agents, documents your terms for disputes, and separates the welcome from the refused in writing. Stopping agents that ignore policy is enforcement, a different layer (see AgentBlock). Declaration plus enforcement is the complete posture; either alone is half.
