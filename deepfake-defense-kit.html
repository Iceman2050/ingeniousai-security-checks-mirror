Step 1 — Generate your family code word

A code word is the one defense a voice clone cannot beat: a secret decided in person, asked on any panicked call. Generate one now, then tell every family member face-to-face. Never text it. Never email it.

The one-page protocol

  1. Who knows it: every family member, decided and spoken in person. If someone was not there, they do not have it — brief them directly, never over text or email.
  2. When to use it: any caller — no matter how familiar the voice — who claims an emergency and wants money, information, or secrecy. Ask: "What's our code word?"
  3. If they cannot answer: the call is over. Hang up immediately. Then call the real person on a phone number you already have saved — never the number the caller gives you. If you cannot reach them, call another relative.
  4. Never do on that call: no wire transfers, no crypto, no gift cards, no OTP or PIN codes. A real emergency survives ten minutes of verification.

Why a code word, really?


The scam the FTC describes works because love is faster than doubt: a panicked voice that sounds like your grandson, an arrest or a wreck, money needed right now, and — critically — do not tell anyone else. Every element is engineered to stop you verifying. A code word agreed in advance breaks the spell in one question, because no cloning tool can guess a secret it has never heard. It is analog, it is free, and it is the one check that scales from children to grandparents.


What to do if it already happened: this is not carelessness — the call is designed to make a caring person act before they can think. If money was sent, contact the bank or transfer service immediately, then report to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. Speed matters.





FAQ — deepfake family protection


What does deepfake mean in the context of phone scams?


A deepfake uses AI to imitate a real person's voice or face. In phone scams, attackers clone a loved one's voice from a short audio clip — the FTC warns the clone can sound exactly like them, so the voice alone is no longer proof of identity.


Can you detect a deepfake phone call by listening?


Not reliably. There may be small tells — odd pauses or unnatural phrasing — but AI voices are now close enough that listening is not a safe test. The defense is verification: hang up, call back on a saved number, and ask for your family's code word.


How do I set up a family code word against AI voice scams?


Choose a word or phrase everyone in the family knows, decided in person and never shared by text or email. When any caller claims an emergency, ask for the code word. If they cannot give it, hang up and call the real person on a number you already have saved.


What should I do if an urgent caller demands secrecy and money?


Secrecy plus urgency plus a hard-to-reverse payment (wire, crypto, gift cards) is the classic scam pattern. Do not send anything on that call. Hang up, verify the story through a separate channel, and report the attempt to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
