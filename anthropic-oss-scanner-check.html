Cybersecurity News: Anthropic's Free AI Scanner Hunts Bugs in Open Source

On October 8, 2026, Anthropic launched OSS Scanner: a free, opt-in AI vulnerability scanner for open-source projects, powered by its strongest models including Claude Mythos. It grew out of Project Glasswing, which surfaced 29,000+ candidate vulnerabilities in six months — a volume humans could never triage. Would AI find the vulnerability in your open-source project?

What is Anthropic's OSS Scanner?

OSS Scanner is an opt-in service: enrolled open-source projects receive thorough, periodic security scans by Anthropic's strongest models at no cost. Each report includes an explanation of the vulnerability, a self-contained reproducer, a bisection of when the bug entered the code (when possible), and a candidate patch. It is informed by Project Glasswing, Anthropic's research program that surfaced over 29,000 candidate vulnerabilities in six months — of which only about 6,000 could be manually triaged. That triage bottleneck is exactly what OSS Scanner removes: reports are fully model-generated, with no human review or triage before they reach maintainers.

The trade-off, stated by Anthropic itself: no human review means faster and more frequent scanning — but some findings may be incorrect or invalid. A few maintainers have noted inflated severity ratings or misunderstood threat models. Maintainers must validate every report before acting on it.

Why this cybersecurity news matters: the validation numbers

To validate the scanner, Anthropic's penetration testers reviewed 97 critical and high-severity findings across 48 projects:

  • 85 (88%) met the bar for coordinated vulnerability disclosure
  • 11 were real vulnerabilities but duplicated already-known issues
  • 1 was a false positive

Early participants back it up. wolfSSL's Todd Ouska said the project received 74 reports, all but two valid, with five becoming CVEs. curl creator Daniel Stenberg said the scanner surfaced one of the worst curl vulnerabilities in years. A PostgreSQL maintainer said an unusually high fraction of findings uncovered real defects.

How projects enroll — and the context

Core maintainers enroll by opening a pull request on Anthropic's OSS Scanner GitHub repository with a YAML config (repo link, primary contact email) and a Dockerfile that builds the project offline so an agent can audit it without internet access. Eligibility mirrors Google's OSS-Fuzz: projects must have critical infrastructure or user-security impact — selective, not universal. 116 projects applied in launch week.

Context worth noting: OSS Scanner is one piece of Anthropic's broader "Cyber Mission," alongside a Critical Infrastructure Defense Program for operational technology in utilities. The same company that warned about self-replicating AI worms spreading through smart TVs is now deploying AI to find vulnerabilities — two sides of one strategy: AI changes both the attack surface and the defense.
