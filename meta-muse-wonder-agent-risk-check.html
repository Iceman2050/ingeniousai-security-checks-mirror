What Meta actually launched (verified)

  • Oct 12, 2026: Meta launched Muse Wonder and the stronger Muse Wonder Full, calling them its most capable models for digital assistants. Internal codename: Watermelon.
  • The behavior change: AI chief Alexandr Wang says Wonder is specifically trained to try other approaches when it hits a roadblock — website restrictions, missing info, unavailable options — rather than stalling and pinging the user. Why "it tries harder" is a security story

1. Retries multiply unsupervised decisions

A conventional assistant stops and asks. Wonder's whole pitch is that it doesn't — it picks another route. Each improvisation is an action you didn't review: a fallback vendor charged, a different contact messaged, a permission used in a way you didn't foresee.

2. Persistent memory is a persistent target

Active Memory builds a standing profile of your preferences, routines, and history. That's a data store — one that can leak in a breach, surface in the wrong context, or be extracted via prompt injection. If you can't see and delete what it remembers, you don't control it.

3. Safety as a paid tier

Reserving the strongest cyber-misuse protections for paying users means the free tier — where most users live — runs with weaker guardrails against exactly the abuse agentic AI enables.

4. The permission question is already live

Users alleging the app touched personal messages without permission (disputed by Meta) is a preview of the core agentic risk: an assistant with broad access deciding for itself what "helping" permits.

Frequently asked questions

What are the security risks of AI agents?

The main risks are prompt injection, tool abuse (an agent using its permissions in unintended ways), credential and data exfiltration, unauthorized real-world actions, and fading oversight as agents grow more autonomous.

How do you stop an AI agent from going rogue?

Use least-privilege permissions, require human approval for irreversible actions, enforce egress allowlists at the network layer rather than in the prompt, keep tamper-evident logs of every agent action, and maintain a working kill switch.
