Phishing quiz FAQ

Is this phishing quiz free?

Yes. All 10 rounds, the red-flag breakdowns, and your score card are free with no signup and nothing installed.

Are the phishing examples real?

No. Every scenario is an original simulated example written for training. Real phishing emails are never reproduced, and no real brands' messages are copied.

What makes a message likely phishing?

The most reliable signals are a sender domain that does not match the claimed brand, a link whose real destination differs from its label, manufactured urgency or threats, and requests for credentials or payments through the message itself.

What should I do if I clicked a real phishing link?

Disconnect, do not enter anything, change the password from a known-good device starting with your email account, enable two-factor authentication, and report the message to your IT team or email provider.

What should I do if I get a suspicious email?

Don't click links or open attachments. Verify the sender through a separate channel — type the site's address yourself or call a known number — then report it to your IT team or your country's phishing-report address, and delete it.

What happens if an employee clicks a phishing link?

It depends on what happens next: merely loading a page rarely compromises a device, but entering credentials hands the attacker your account. Act fast — change the password from a clean device, revoke sessions, enable two-factor authentication, and alert IT so they can check for lateral movement.
