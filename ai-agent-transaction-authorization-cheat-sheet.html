Key facts

  • 4 fields make a receipt: who approved, which agent, how much, and what scope was declared — recorded before the action, not reconstructed after.
  • Receipts are hash-chained: each approval seals into a SHA-256 chain, so a missing or edited approval breaks verification.
  • Approval precedes action: a record created after the agent acted is a confession, not an authorisation.

What is an agent transaction authorization in agentic ai?

An agent transaction authorization is the pre-action record that a specific human approved a specific agent to do a specific thing for a specific amount. It answers, before money or commitments move, the question every incident asks afterwards: who said this agent could do that? Without the record, the honest answer is usually that nobody can prove anyone did.

  • Approver: the named human who authorised the action — not a team, not a role, a person.
  • Agent: which agent, which run, acting under which configuration.
  • Amount: the value ceiling approved — the receipt authorises up to this, never beyond.
  • Declared scope: what the approval covers, in words the approver actually saw: payee class, purpose, window of validity.

What is the 5-step authorization ceremony?

  1. Declare: the agent states the intended action — what, to whom, how much, why — before doing anything.
  2. Approve: the named human reviews the declaration as presented and approves or denies it.
  3. Seal: the approval is written to the hash-chained ledger, timestamped and tamper-evident.
  4. Act: only now does the agent execute — within the amount and scope sealed in the receipt.
  5. Verify: after execution, the action is checked against the receipt; any excess is a finding, not a rounding error.

What goes wrong without receipts?

  • Scope drift: approvals given in chat scroll away; the agent's tenth action inherits the first one's blessing.
  • Amount creep: without a sealed ceiling, 'the usual order' quietly grows.
  • Unprovable innocence: when a payment is disputed, the team that did everything right but kept no receipts looks exactly like the team that did nothing.
  • Shared-accountability fog: if everyone approved in general, no one approved in particular.

Frequently asked questions

What is agentic ai transaction authorization?

It is recording, before an AI agent acts, exactly who approved the action: the approver, the agent, the amount and the declared scope. Agentic Receipts seals each approval into a SHA-256 hash-chained ledger, so the authorisation can be verified later and cannot be quietly edited or backdated.

Why must approval happen before the agent acts?

Because an after-the-fact record cannot authorise anything — it can only describe. Sequencing is the control: declare, approve, seal, then act, then verify against the receipt. If the seal does not precede the action in the ledger's own timestamps and chain, the approval did not govern the action.

What is a hash-chained ledger in simple terms?

Each record's hash is computed over the previous record's hash plus its own contents. Editing or deleting any earlier approval breaks every later hash, so tampering is detectable on verification. It gives a small team's approval log the tamper evidence that disputes and audits actually require.
