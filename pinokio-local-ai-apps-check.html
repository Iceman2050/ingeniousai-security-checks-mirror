What is Pinokio?

A one-click installer that runs open-source AI apps on your own computer — no cloud needed — by packaging models and scripts into simple launchers.

Pinokio (pinokio.co) is a free, open-source “AI browser” that installs and runs open-source AI apps — image generators, text-to-speech, video tools, local LLMs — on your own computer with one click: no terminal, no Python setup, no dependency hell. Each app lands in its own isolated environment, and because everything runs locally, your data stays on your machine. No account required, no paid tier. The repo (pinokiocomputer/pinokio) is MIT-licensed with 8,200+ stars and was updated as recently as this week. The pitch is real. But here is the sentence the pitch hurries past: a Pinokio script can run anything on your computer — it is, by the project's own description, a user-friendly terminal. One click is convenient precisely because it is powerful. This 60-second check tests whether your setup — and your habits — are ready for it. Free, no signup.

The trust question: who vets your one click?

  • The Discover catalog is curated. Per the project's script policy, Discover scripts are manually listed, tracked via Git, and frozen under the official pinokiocomputer organization — the closest thing to a safety guarantee in this model.
  • The catalog is now community-powered. Since June 2026, anyone can publish launchers, with GitHub-verified ownership marking “official updates.” More choice, more responsibility on you.
  • Isolation is about dependencies, not your data. Each app gets its own environment (no pip/conda conflicts), but a running script executes with your user privileges — isolation won't stop a malicious launcher reading your files.
  • 475 open issues. Real users hit real failures: broken installs, GPU quirks, version drift. “No dependency errors” is a goal, not a guarantee — the community tab exists precisely because things break.
  • Privacy is app-dependent. Pinokio itself asks for nothing, but each app you install is its own software with its own network behavior. Local-first is a strong default, not a blanket promise.
  • The GPU tax is real. The launcher pages say it plainly: NVIDIA-only builds, 6GB+ VRAM minimums, ~63GB pruned weight downloads. Check the requirements before the download, not after.
