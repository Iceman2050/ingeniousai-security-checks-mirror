Why can't you see most of your own traffic?

Because the measurement stack was built for people. Analytics tools run a script in a browser; bots do not run scripts, so the entire machine majority is filtered out before reporting begins. What remains is presented as 'your traffic' without the caveat that it is the human 42.5%. Server logs and edge data are the only complete record.

Frequently asked questions

What are AI agents in web traffic?

In web traffic, 'AI agents' covers the automated visitors from AI systems: training crawlers collecting content, AI search crawlers fetching pages to answer questions, and transactional agents browsing or buying for users. Together with other bots they passed human traffic in 2026: 57.5% of HTML requests, per Cloudflare. AgentBlock classifies the known AI ones by user-agent.

How much of my traffic is AI agents?

It varies, and the honest answer is: measure it. Network-wide, bots were 57.5% of HTML traffic in June 2026 (Cloudflare), and on small-business sites AI crawlers alone made 40.7% of identified bot requests (LovedByAI, 924 sites). Your mix is in your server logs, grouped by user-agent, waiting to be counted.

Why not block every bot?

Because bots are not one thing. Search crawlers send your customers, AI search crawlers increasingly do the same, and transactional agents may arrive ready to buy. Training crawlers are the usual block target: they take content and return nothing. A split policy beats a blanket one in both directions.

Can AgentBlock stop a scraper pretending to be a browser?

No, and be suspicious of any tool that promises it. Classification works on declared identity: known agent user-agents. A scraper that forges a browser identity needs other defenses, such as rate limits and behavior analysis. AgentBlock's honest scope is the declared majority, which is most of the volume and all of the reputable crawlers.
