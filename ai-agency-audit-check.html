AGENTIC AI — PERMISSION AUDIT

First they took your attention. Now they want your agency.

Nobel Peace Prize laureate Maria Ressa describes AI's takeover in three phases: social media cloned you and microtargeted your attention; LLM chatbots moved into your intimacy; and now agentic AI — systems you give permission to act for you — puts something deeper at stake. Her framing is argument, not incident report. But the practical question it raises is real and answerable: who currently has permission to act as you?

"We gave up our attention, we gave up our intimacy, now what's being hacked is agency itself. These agents — literally you give them permission to act for you."— Maria Ressa, Foreign Policy interview, September 2026 (reel transcript; her characterization)

Short answer: every "Sign in with Google" click, every shopping or scheduling agent, every API key and OAuth grant is a piece of your agency you've delegated. Most people have dozens they forgot. Audit them tonight: revoke what you don't recognize, narrow what you keep, and put approval steps on anything that can spend money.

The 10-minute audit — where to look

1. OAuth grants (the biggest pile)

Google: myaccount.google.com → Security → Third-party access. Microsoft: account.microsoft.com → Privacy → Apps and services. Apple: Settings → your name → Sign in with Apple. Remove anything you don't recognize or no longer use. Full Gmail/Drive access for a coupon app is a revoke.

2. AI agents with standing permission

Shopping agents, scheduling assistants, coding agents, "auto-pilot" tools — list every agent that can act without asking you first. If one was compromised or went rogue, what could it do right now? That's your blast radius.

3. Broad API access

API keys, "full access" integrations, email plugins that can read and send. Narrow scopes to the minimum the tool needs; rotate keys you haven't touched in months.

4. Data retention on your chatbot

Check what your most-used AI chatbot stores about you and whether conversations train future models. Delete or opt out where the option exists.

5. Automated spending

Subscriptions, saved payment methods, one-click buying, agents with checkout permission. Set spending caps and require approval above a threshold you choose.

Reading Ressa's claim fairly

  • What it is: a Nobel laureate's argument about power and attention, delivered as commentary — not a vulnerability disclosure or incident report.
  • What's verifiable: the three phases she names (microtargeting era, LLM chatbots ~2022–23, agentic systems now) match the industry's own timeline; "probabilistic, never factual" is an accurate description of how LLMs work.
  • What's arguable: whether "agency itself" is being "hacked," or whether delegation to agents is a trade users knowingly make. You don't have to agree with her politics to benefit from the audit.
  • The actionable core: regardless of the framing, unreviewed delegated permissions are a genuine security surface — compromised OAuth grants and rogue agents are documented attack paths.
