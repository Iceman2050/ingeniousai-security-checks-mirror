Web scraping at scale: what the 924-site study found

The LovedByAI study read a month of server logs from 924 small-business websites, the kind of sites that assume they are beneath notice. AI crawlers made 40.7% of identified bot page requests, level with all search engines combined at 40.8%. On 79% of the sites, AI crawlers out-requested Googlebot, at a median ratio of 3.1 AI-crawler requests for every one from Google.

The most active single crawler was Meta-ExternalAgent at 21.6% of AI-crawler requests, ahead of ClaudeBot at 18.5% and GPTBot at 12.2%. None of this appears in ordinary analytics, because bots do not run the tracking script. Why does robots.txt not stop AI scraping?

Because robots.txt is a convention, not a control. It publishes your wishes in a file honest crawlers read and honor. A training scraper that ignores it loses nothing: the file cannot refuse a request, throttle it or even reliably notice it. Policy lives in robots.txt; enforcement has to live at the edge, in the path of the request itself.

  1. Read the logs first: name your top AI crawlers and their volumes for the last month. Policy written blind is policy written wrong.

Frequently asked questions

What is AI web scraping?

AI web scraping is the automated collection of site content by AI companies' crawlers, mainly to train models and to answer questions in AI search. It runs at industrial scale: a study of 924 small-business sites counted a median 1,050 AI-crawler requests per site per month in autumn 2026. AgentBlock exists to let site owners decide which of those agents get served.

Does robots.txt block AI crawlers?

robots.txt declares your preferences; it cannot enforce them. Honest crawlers from major AI companies generally honor it, and it is worth publishing as your public policy. Scrapers that ignore it lose nothing, which is why enforcement at the edge, classifying user-agents in the request path, is the layer that makes the policy real.

Which AI crawlers visit a typical site?

In the LovedByAI study of 924 small-business sites (September-October 2026), the most active was Meta-ExternalAgent at 21.6% of AI-crawler requests, then ClaudeBot at 18.5% and GPTBot at 12.2%. OpenAI's crawlers together took the largest company share at 27.2%. The median site saw 11 different AI crawlers in a month.

Should I block all AI crawlers?

Not necessarily, and AgentBlock does not force one answer. Training crawlers take content and return nothing; AI search crawlers can send visitors; transactional agents may become customers. Most owners land on a split policy: block training, allow search, decide agents case by case. What matters is that the decision is yours and enforced.
