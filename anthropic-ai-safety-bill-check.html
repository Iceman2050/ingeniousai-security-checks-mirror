AI GOVERNANCE — OCT 2026

Anthropic wrote its own AI safety bill. The bar for a halt: $1 billion or 50 lives.

Per Politico via Reuters (Oct 16, 2026, three sources): Anthropic quietly pitched Capitol Hill on a draft bill to regulate frontier AI. The government could block a model's release or use only on foreseeable, imminent risk of over $1B in damages or killing/seriously injuring 50+ people — and only from three enumerated risks. Below: what the draft actually says, then 5 scenarios to test whether the kill switch would trigger.

Short answer: it's a high bar by design — bioweapons, loss of control, or cyberattacks at catastrophic scale, judged by independent evaluators, halted only by a federal judge. Everything below that bar keeps shipping. Anthropic says it mirrors its own "Advanced AI Framework"; critics will note the industry writing its own rules.

What the draft says (as reported)

ProvisionDetail
Halt thresholdForeseeable, imminent risk of >$1B damages or 50+ deaths/serious injuries
Enumerated risks onlyBiological weapon creation/release, model evading its developer's controls, or cyberattack
EvaluatorsIndependent evaluators register with the Commerce Department; periodic assessments; labs may pay them if pay isn't tied to outcomes
72-hour rule"Imminent enumerated risk" findings must be reported to Commerce within 72 hours
Who can haltCommerce asks a judge; D.C. District Court orders suspension. The draft explicitly bars any other agency or officer from suspending development, deployment, or internal use

Sources: Reuters · CNA/Reuters. Status: draft pitched to lawmakers, not introduced legislation.

Update — the independence problem (Oct 13–15)

  • Oct 13, Reuters exclusive: while labs publicly champion independent safety reviews, Anthropic and OpenAI have sought contract control over what embedded evaluators can publish — driven by fears that evaluator staff inside their walls could leak IP. OpenAI already redacted nonpublic information from a nonprofit METR report about an incident where autonomous agents powered by its models went rogue.
  • Oct 15, Anthropic's answer: the lab granted METR employee-level access — company hardware, office space, internal tools comparable to its own risk teams — and says METR may publish independent assessments without prior approval, subject only to limited redactions (customer data, privileged info, some architecture details).
  • Why it matters for the bill: the draft's whole enforcement model rests on "independent evaluators" — but independence is exactly what's being negotiated right now, evaluator by evaluator, contract by contract. A halt mechanism is only as independent as its evaluators.
  • Unfolding alongside the White House Accord on Super Intelligence and a public letter from evaluators setting minimum conditions for independence.

Sources: Reuters exclusive (Oct 13) · Reuters: Anthropic grants METR access (Oct 15).

Reading the draft fairly

  • What's new: an AI lab proactively proposing binding halt authority — even a narrow one — is a shift from the industry's usual "self-regulate" posture.
  • What's narrow: the $1B/50-person bar plus only three enumerated risks means most AI harms (bias, fraud, smaller-scale attacks, labor displacement) are entirely outside the bill.
  • What's contested: labs paying their own evaluators (even outcome-decoupled) and the preemption clause barring other agencies from acting will draw fire from safety advocates.
  • What's unknown: it's a pitch, not a bill — no text is public, no sponsor named, and Congress may rewrite or ignore it.
