What makes a store agent-shoppable?

Agents don’t browse — they parse. A store an agent can buy from has six things: a machine-readable product feed (not just rendered HTML), structured product data on every page, real-time inventory and pricing, a checkout with no human-only gates, a published agent policy stating which agents may transact and on what terms, and machine-readable returns, shipping and cancellation terms. Miss one, and the agent either guesses — bad — or shops elsewhere.

What is an agent-shoppable store?

An agent-shoppable store is one an AI agent can actually buy from: machine-readable catalog, structured product data, real-time inventory, a checkout without human-only gates, published agent terms, and machine-readable policies. It is a data property of your store, not a feature you buy — any platform can have it, any platform can lack it.

Frequently asked questions

Which stores signed deals with Meta for agent purchases?

Per October 2026 press reporting, Meta signed partnerships with Walmart, Gap, Sephora and Best Buy allowing its Muse assistant to make purchases on their websites. Shopify separately opened its network of online stores to AI agents. The pattern is deals, not open doors — agents transact where terms were negotiated.

What is agentic shopping?

Agentic shopping is commerce where an AI agent acts on a shopper’s behalf: it discovers products, compares options, and completes the purchase without the human clicking through each step. Juniper Research estimates agentic commerce at $8 billion in transaction value in 2026, growing toward $3.5 trillion by 2031.

What is an agent-shoppable store?

An agent-shoppable store is one an AI agent can actually buy from: machine-readable product feeds, structured product data, real-time inventory and pricing, a checkout without human-only gates, published agent terms, and machine-readable returns policies. If any link is missing, the agent stalls or shops elsewhere.

Do I need Shopify to accept AI agent shoppers?

No. Shopify opening its network to agents is one route, but the underlying requirements are platform-neutral: structured product data, a machine-readable catalog, and a checkout an agent can complete. Any store platform can meet them — and any store platform can fail them.

Will AI agents really buy from small stores?

They buy where the data lets them. Agents don’t prefer big brands — they prefer complete, accurate, machine-readable information. A small store with clean structured data and real-time inventory is more agent-shoppable than a giant with a messy catalog. During Cyber Week 2025, Salesforce reported 1 in 5 orders involved an AI agent.

What blocks AI agents at checkout most often?

Human-only gates: CAPTCHAs, phone-verification steps, and logins that require a person. Next come stale inventory (the agent buys what isn’t in stock), missing structured pricing, and terms buried in prose the agent can’t parse.
