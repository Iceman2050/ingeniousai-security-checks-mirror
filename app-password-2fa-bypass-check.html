Revoke them in two minutes

Gmail / Google

Go to myaccount.google.com/apppasswords (signed in; it will ask for your 2FA), find the app password by creation date, and remove it. Prefer OAuth sign-in for the app afterwards; store any replacement in a secret manager.

Apple

Go to appleid.apple.com → Sign-In and Security → App-Specific Passwords, and revoke — or revoke all if you cannot tell which is which. Store any replacement in a secret manager or the macOS Keychain.

Then

Rotate every downstream service that used the password (CI jobs, mail clients, backup scripts) — revocation breaks them by design. Move secrets to runtime-injected environment variables or a secret manager. A .env file copied around is a credential leak waiting to happen.

Frequently asked questions

What is an app-specific password?

A long, machine-generated password issued by Google or Apple so a third-party app (an old mail client, a backup script, a CI mailer) can sign in to your account. Gmail app passwords are 16 lowercase letters; Apple app-specific passwords are four groups of four letters/digits joined by hyphens.

How does an app password bypass 2FA?

By design: app passwords are scoped credentials that authenticate without triggering your second factor. If one leaks, whoever holds it signs in as you — no authenticator prompt, no SMS, no passkey. That is why they are high-value targets.

What did MITRE ATT&CK v20 add about app passwords?

Per October 2026 news coverage, MITRE ATT&CK v20 added nine new Enterprise techniques including one for stealing application-specific passwords — formalizing what red teams already knew: these credentials are a distinct, prized attack target. (Reported by letsdatascience.com; cited as news coverage, not independently verified.)

Where do app passwords usually leak?

Dotfiles (~/.bashrc, ~/.zshrc), .env files, CI/CD configs, password-manager exports, notes apps, chat history, screenshots, and old laptop backups. They persist for years because nothing reminds you they exist.

How do I revoke a Gmail app password?

Go to myaccount.google.com/apppasswords while signed in (it will ask for your 2FA), find the app password by its creation date, and remove it. Then update the app: prefer OAuth sign-in, and store any replacement in a secret manager — never back in the file.

How do I revoke an Apple app-specific password?

Go to appleid.apple.com, open Sign-In and Security, then App-Specific Passwords, and revoke the password — or revoke all if you cannot tell which is which. Generate a replacement only if an app still needs one, and store it in a secret manager or the macOS Keychain.
