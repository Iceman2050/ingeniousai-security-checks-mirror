The 6-question self-test

Answer for your AI-generated ads and content that reaches audiences in the EU.

1. Do you run AI-generated or AI-manipulated ads — images, video, or audio a viewer could mistake for real?

2. Is the AI origin disclosed on the ad itself, at first exposure — not just in metadata or a buried caption?

3. Do any ads use AI-generated faces, voices, or virtual influencers (deepfakes)?

4. Is your AI content machine-readable-marked (watermark or C2PA-style provenance)?

5. Do you publish AI-generated text to inform the public (news, health, policy, environment) without genuine human editorial review?

6. Do you run AI chatbots or AI customer agents without clearly telling users they are talking to AI?

Frequently asked questions

Do AI-generated ads need a label in the EU?

Some do. Visible disclosure becomes particularly relevant when an AI-generated or manipulated advertisement realistically depicts a person, object, product, place, entity, or event and could falsely appear authentic or truthful. Advertising content is excluded from the lighter disclosure regime for genuinely artistic or satirical work.

Does every AI-assisted marketing asset need a disclosure label?

No. Standard editing tasks fall outside the marking duty, including grammar correction, spellchecking, minor stylistic polish, and AI-generated translations. Summaries and substantive rewrites still require marking. Visible disclosure is triggered by deepfake content and by unreviewed public-interest text.

What happens if you ignore the AI content disclosure requirements?

Breaching the Article 50 transparency obligations carries fines of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. The AI Act's steepest band — up to €35 million or 7% of global turnover — applies to prohibited AI practices under Article 5.

Does AI-written marketing copy need a disclaimer?

Purely promotional copy may sit outside the disclosure rules, but content that informs the public on regulatory, technical, health or environmental matters is more likely to be flagged. Genuine human authorship and editorial ownership are the cleanest way to stay on the right side of the line.

Do the EU AI Act disclosure rules apply to companies outside the EU?

Yes, they can. The Act follows the AI output rather than the company's location: if your AI system's output is used in the European Union — for example AI-generated campaign assets reaching EU audiences — you can be in scope wherever you are established.
