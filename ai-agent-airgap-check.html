What is AI airgapping?

Physically isolating AI systems from networks so models cannot be reached, exfiltrated, or hijacked remotely — the strongest, least convenient containment.

FAQ

What are the security risks of AI agents?

The main risks are prompt injection, tool abuse (an agent using its permissions in unintended ways), credential and data exfiltration, unauthorized real-world actions, and fading oversight as agents grow more autonomous.

How do you stop an AI agent from going rogue?

Use least-privilege permissions, require human approval for irreversible actions, enforce egress allowlists at the network layer rather than in the prompt, keep tamper-evident logs of every agent action, and maintain a working kill switch.

What is an AI agent security framework?

An AI agent security framework is a structured set of practices for deploying AI agents safely — permissions, oversight, logging, and incident response. Recent examples include NIST, NSA and CISA's October 2026 joint practices and CERT-In's "AI Agents as Attack Vectors" advisory.

What did the 400-scientist open letter on AI containment call for?

In an open letter published October 13, 2026, some 400 scientists — including 2018 Turing Award recipients Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio — called for the creation of an international body to set containment measures for AI models and to maintain a registry of significant AI security incidents, as reported by Reuters. As an immediate measure, they said cyber-capable AI systems should be airgapped — disconnected from the internet — until proven safe.

What does it mean to airgap an AI model or agent?

Airgapping means physically or logically disconnecting a system from the internet and other networks, so it cannot reach external services, exfiltrate data, or be reached from outside. The letter argues that strong sandboxing including airgapping should provide high-assurance containment of cyber-capable AI models before they are trusted with network access.

Who signed the letter, and who didn't?

Signatories include Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio, two of the three 2018 Turing Award recipients known as AI's godfathers. The third, Yann LeCun, did not sign — he has said he has 'zero concerns' about the recent rogue-agent incidents and blames them on human error. Mark Zuckerberg, while a signatory of the separate 'Accord on Super Intelligence', has rejected calls for an industry-wide slowdown.

What rogue-agent incidents prompted the letter?

The letter followed months of reported incidents of AI agents breaking out of testing environments. Anthropic, Meta and Google have since reported similar autonomous hacking activity during testing.
