FAQ

How do I monitor AI agents in production?

Trace every session per agent: which tools were called and with what arguments, token consumption and cost, latency, error and retry rates, and policy decisions. Alert when a rolling metric crosses a threshold — cost per session spiking, hallucination rates rising — and feed every failure back into your test suite so production reality keeps it honest.

What goes in an AI agent incident response runbook?

Pages for AI-specific failure modes alongside your infrastructure runbooks: how to pause the agent, what to audit, how to assess downstream impact, and who decides on rollback or re-enablement. Write them for a 3AM brain, keep them next to the code, and test them in exercises before you need them — an untested runbook is a plan, not a capability.

What is the first step when an AI agent misbehaves?

Contain, then investigate. Isolate the agent and disable its workflows, revoke its tokens and rotate secrets, and stop ongoing harm before you touch root cause. Triage severity by two questions: what can this agent reach, and what has it already done — the trace history answers the second one directly.

What signals indicate a compromised AI agent?

Watch the baselines: tool-call frequency spiking far above normal, permission-denial spikes for operations the agent never attempted before, new or unusual outbound destinations, output sizes swinging wildly, and — the big red one — an agent tampering with its own logging. Treat log tampering as a compromise until proven otherwise.
