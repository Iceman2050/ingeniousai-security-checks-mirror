The lesson: length beats complexity

Each extra character multiplies the attacker's work by the size of the character set. That's why the reel's illustrative examples land where they do:

  • abc123 — 6 characters, lowercase + digits → falls instantly offline.
  • P@ssw0rd! — 9 characters with symbols → the math says days, but it's in every breach dictionary, so in practice it falls in seconds. Complexity you can guess isn't complexity.
  • Tr0ub4dor&3 — 11 mixed characters → illustrative estimate of years of brute force… still crackable, still reused everywhere.
  • six random words — ~30+ characters → illustrative estimate of thousands of years. Length wins, and it's easier to remember.

A passphrase like correct horse battery staple is long, memorable, and mathematically brutal. That combination is the whole trick.

Online vs offline cracking

🛡️ Online attackThe attacker guesses against your login form. Rate limits, CAPTCHAs, and account lockouts slow them to a crawl — a few guesses per second at best. This is where most people imagine brute force happening.
☠️ Offline attackThe attacker steals the site's password-hash database, then cracks it on their own hardware. No rate limits. No lockouts. GPU farms try billions of guesses per second. This is the attack that actually empties accounts after a breach.

Once hashes leak, your password's only remaining defense is its own entropy — plus whether the site used a slow hash (below).

The 4 defenses that actually work

  • MFA everywhere. A second factor (authenticator app, hardware key) means a cracked or stolen password alone is useless. This is the single highest-leverage defense you have.
  • Rate-limit + lockout (for site operators). Slow the online attack to a crawl: limit attempts per account and per IP, add delays, lock accounts after repeated failures.
  • Slow hashing (for developers). Store passwords with bcrypt or argon2, not fast hashes. They can be tuned to take a fraction of a second per guess — collapsing an offline attacker's speed from billions to thousands of guesses per second.
  • Long, unique passphrases + a password manager. One long passphrase per site, generated and stored by a password manager. Reuse is what turns one breach into ten.

Strong Password FAQ

Why does password length matter more than complexity?

Every extra character multiplies the search space by the size of the character set. Adding one character to a password is roughly equivalent to squaring the attacker's work — length grows the number of combinations exponentially, while complexity only changes the base. A long passphrase of ordinary words beats a short password with symbols.

What is offline password cracking?

When attackers steal a database of password hashes, they can crack them on their own hardware with no rate limits and no lockouts — GPU farms try billions of guesses per second. This is why even a password that would take years against a rate-limited login form can fall quickly once the hash database leaks.

Why is MFA the single best defense?

Multi-factor authentication adds a second, independent proof of identity — typically something you have (your phone) alongside something you know (your password). Even if an attacker brute-forces or steals your password, they still cannot log in without the second factor.

What makes bcrypt and argon2 different from fast hashes?

Bcrypt and argon2 are deliberately slow and memory-intensive. A fast hash like MD5 or SHA-256 lets an attacker try billions of guesses per second on a GPU; bcrypt/argon2 can be tuned to take a fraction of a second per guess, collapsing an attacker's offline cracking speed from billions to thousands per second.

Should I test my real password in the estimator above?

No. Never type a password you actually use into any website — including this one. Test a password shaped like yours instead (same length, same mix of character types). The estimator runs entirely in your browser and sends nothing anywhere, but the habit of never typing real passwords anywhere is the protection.
