CRITICAL THINKING

Can You Still Think Without AI?

A question worth sitting with before your next prompt: if the AI went away tomorrow, would your judgment still work?

"The real AI divide may not be between those who can use AI and those who cannot. It may be between those who can still think critically without it and those who cannot."
— paraphrased from Nomrota Sarker's op-ed "When we outsource thinking to AI, what happens to human judgment?", The Daily Star, October 14, 2026

The paradox

Sarker's point, in short: we are building machines that process vast information, generate arguments, and imitate reasoning — while many societies rushing to adopt them struggle to teach the skills needed to question their answers: literacy, numeracy, critical thinking, media literacy, civic reasoning.

The divide that matters, the piece argues, isn't access to the tool. It's whether the habit of independent judgment survives the tool.

What the research actually says

The cognitive-offloading study

Researcher Michael Gerlich at SBS Swiss Business School surveyed 666 UK respondents and conducted 50 in-depth interviews. The findings, in plain language:

  • AI use and critical thinking moved in opposite directions (correlation r = −0.68) — heavier AI users scored lower on critical-thinking measures.
  • Cognitive offloading — handing memory and problem-solving over to devices — was strongly linked to AI use (r = 0.72) and inversely linked to critical thinking (r = −0.75).
  • Mediation analysis suggested roughly 60% of AI use's negative link to critical thinking ran through offloading: the tool doesn't dull you directly — the habit of never doing the work yourself does.
  • Ages 17–25 used AI the most and scored lowest; those 46+ used it least and scored highest.
  • In interviews, respondents with advanced degrees tended to cross-check AI answers; less-schooled respondents tended to follow the tool.

Honest limits: this is one study. It used self-report measures, and a correlation is not proof of causation — it could be that weaker critical thinkers reach for AI more, not (only) that AI weakens thinking. Treat it as evidence, not a verdict.

Why this is a security question

Scammers now write with the same fluent, confident AI you do. The phishing email, the fake invoice, the "urgent" message from your "boss," the deepfaked voice of your grandchild — they all read perfectly. Fluency is no longer a signal of truth.

That means the defense against AI-powered deception is exactly the habit Sarker describes: independent verification. Cross-check. Look for the source. Ask for the opposite argument. The person who verifies survives the fluent lie; the person who outsources judgment clicks it.

Critical thinking isn't just an academic virtue anymore. It's the firewall.
