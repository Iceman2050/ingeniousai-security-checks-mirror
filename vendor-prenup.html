FAQ

What is AI vendor lock-in?

Lock-in is the cost of leaving: data that only exists inside one provider's systems, workflows built on provider-specific features, contracts with renewal traps, and staff who only know one API. It's not that switching is impossible — it's that the longer you wait to plan it, the more expensive it gets.

How do I export my data from an AI vendor?

Start with an inventory of everything you store there — prompts, conversation logs, fine-tunes, embeddings, API keys and configs. Export through the provider's dashboard or API, verify completeness by spot-checking record counts, save a copy in a neutral format, and confirm deletion after you've migrated. This tool generates the per-vendor steps for your setup above.

What contract traps should I check in an AI vendor agreement?

Auto-renewal clauses and how notice must be given; what happens to your data after termination (retention vs. deletion, and on whose servers); egress or extraction fees; price-change notice periods; minimum spend commitments; and what survives termination — confidentiality and data obligations usually do. Verify each one in your contract; generic summaries are no substitute.

In what order should I switch AI vendors?

Least-integrated first. Move the vendor that only does one shallow job — the easiest to replace and the least risky to test — and use that migration to prove your export process works. Save the deeply embedded provider, the one your whole product routes through, for last, when your team has already made the mistakes on the easy ones.

How often should I review my AI vendor exit plan?

At least yearly, and immediately after any trigger event: a price change, a model deprecation announcement, a terms-of-service update, or a new integration that deepens the dependency. TermsWatch automates the terms-monitoring part of that review.
