CAREER — AI READINESS

90% of enterprise workers aren't AI savvy. Are you?

Palo Alto Networks CEO Nikesh Arora calls this a "Darwinian moment" for careers: AI is redefining roles, and the workers who evolve will be the ones companies keep. His number — 90% not AI savvy — is his assessment from the 20VC podcast, not a census. But the test he describes is real: can you work with AI, or are you waiting to be worked around? Five honest questions.

"The challenge right now is 90% of the enterprise employees are not AI savvy… I think we're back to a Darwinian moment where everybody has to figure out who's really good."— Nikesh Arora, CEO of Palo Alto Networks, on the 20VC podcast (2026)

Short answer: Arora's bar isn't "knows AI trivia" — it's whether AI is part of how you actually work. Daily use, one automated task, self-driven learning, and the ability to explain your AI-augmented value. That's the 10%.

What Arora actually said (verified)

  • 90% "not AI savvy": his estimate of enterprise employees lacking practical AI skills, blamed on the absence of training that can scale — "there's no training course I can send my 21,000 employees to."
  • Self-learning or else: workers must take responsibility for adapting; companies can't train their way out at the speed AI moves.
  • Against layoffs-first: he criticized CEOs (named: Coinbase, Block) who cut staff instead of retraining — Palo Alto Networks reshapes through natural attrition (~2%/month) and hires AI-fluent talent via hackathons.
  • The plan: transform 20–25% of his workforce within 12 months; "give me three years, I'll have hopefully enough AI savvy people."
  • Context: a 2025 Orgvue study found 39% of employers had already made redundancies due to AI — the pressure Arora describes is already in motion.

Sources: Tekedia · AI Magazine · Business Chief · Complete AI Training. Quotes from the 20VC podcast via these reports.

Four moves that put you in the 10%

1. Use AI in your real work, daily

Not demos — your actual tasks. Drafting, analysis, research, code, planning. Fluency comes from repetition, not courses.

2. Automate one of your own tasks end-to-end

Pick the most repetitive thing you do weekly and automate it. That's the single strongest signal of AI savviness — and it's exactly what employers are selecting for.

3. Learn in public, on your own

Arora's point: no company program will do this for you. Build, share, document. (Our 12-stage builder roadmap is one path.)

4. Be able to explain your AI-augmented value

"Here's what I do with AI that I couldn't do before, and here's what it saves." If you can say that concretely, you're the person they keep.
