The Compliance Report Card

A–F grades for 7 major AI vendors on AI disclosure labeling, data-use transparency, and terms fairness. Every grade carries a receipt — a linked public source. Where the record is silent, we say so.

Methodology

  1. Three criteria, plain definitions. Disclosure labeling: does the vendor mark AI-generated content so ordinary people can spot it? Data-use transparency: is it public what data is collected and whether it trains models? Terms fairness: are policies clear, proportionate, and honestly enforced?
  2. Grades are editorial, not official. Ingenious AI assigned each letter grade as a synthesis of the cited evidence. This is not a certification and not legal advice.
  3. Receipts, not vibes. Every single grade links to a real public source — a law firm's analysis, a vendor's own support page, independent research, or credible reporting.
  4. Silence gets INSUFFICIENT DATA. If no reliable public source evaluates a criterion, we mark it openly instead of inventing a grade.
  5. Snapshot: October 2026. Policies change; re-check the receipts before making decisions.

Report Card FAQ

Are these AI vendor grades official?

No. These are editorial grades from Ingenious AI — not official ratings and not a certification. Every grade is anchored to a linked public source, and where the public record is silent the card says INSUFFICIENT DATA rather than guessing.

What are the three grading criteria?

Disclosure labeling: does the vendor mark AI-generated content so people can identify it. Data-use transparency: is it public how user data is collected and used for training. Terms fairness: are the vendor's policies clear, proportionate, and honestly enforced.

What does INSUFFICIENT DATA mean?

It means no reliable public source was found to evaluate that criterion for that vendor. We would rather mark the gap openly than assign a grade on assumptions.

Why does no vendor score an A?

No vendor yet publishes complete training-data documentation, universal AI-content labeling, and fully fair terms at the same time. Independent research agrees: Stanford's transparency research found no developer near adequate transparency, with the top score at 54 out of 100.

How is this different from the Transparent AI Index?

The Transparent AI Index is a broader 12-vendor transparency matrix. This report card narrows the field to 7 major vendors, issues A–F letter grades per criterion, and attaches a verifiable source receipt to every single grade.
