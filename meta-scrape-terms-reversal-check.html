Why the reversal is not a shield

  • A removed clause is not a guarantee. Meta added the provision unilaterally; it can add it back unilaterally. Terms are not defenses you control.
  • The association outlives the clause. Your sites' Meta Business account associations still exist — the trigger mechanism is intact even if the provision is gone.
  • Pixels don't scrape — they transmit. The October controversy was about crawling. But Meta pixels and SDKs on your site send data directly to Meta regardless of any scraping terms. That's the bigger, quieter channel.
  • robots.txt was never the battlefield. The provision was a contract term, not a crawling behavior — robots.txt could never have bound it. Real defenses are access control and data minimization.
  • Re-check on a schedule. Set a quarterly reminder to re-read Meta's commercial terms. The October 17 reversal is proof that "what the terms said" has a half-life.

Frequently asked questions

How do I stop Meta from scraping my website?

There is no single opt-out button. The practical stack: (1) check whether any of your domains are associated with a Meta Business account — that association was the trigger in the October 2026 terms controversy; (2) block AI crawlers in robots.txt; (3) keep your valuable content behind login or API access; (4) audit which Meta pixels, SDKs, and business tools run on your site; and (5) review Meta's current commercial terms yourself, because terms can change — the October 17, 2026 reversal proved it.

Does Meta use my website for AI training?

Potentially, if your site is associated with a Meta Business account. In October 2026, Meta's commercial terms included a provision permitting scraping of business-account-associated websites for AI training; Meta removed that provision on October 17, 2026 after public backlash. But Meta still trains on broadly public web content and receives whatever your Meta pixels and SDKs send.

What is the Meta AI training scraping opt out?

There is no single formal "Meta AI training scraping opt-out" switch. What happened in October 2026 is that Meta removed a scraping provision from its commercial terms — a reversal, not an opt-out tool. The closest thing to opting out is a combination: disassociate business accounts where feasible, block AI crawlers in robots.txt, keep content behind login, and audit the Meta pixels and SDKs embedded on your site.

Did Meta reverse its scraping terms?

Yes. In mid-October 2026, Meta's commercial terms permitted scraping of websites associated with business accounts for AI training purposes. After public backlash, Meta removed the provision on October 17, 2026. The episode shows terms are not guarantees — defenses you control (robots.txt, login gates, pixel audits) matter more than any clause.

Does robots.txt stop Meta from scraping my site?

robots.txt helps against crawlers that respect it, but it is a request, not a lock. It does nothing about content Meta receives directly through its pixels, SDKs, and business tools, and it never bound the October 2026 commercial-terms provision, which was a contract term, not a crawling behavior. Use robots.txt as one layer — login gates and pixel audits are stronger.

What is an ai.txt file?

An ai.txt file is an emerging convention — modeled on robots.txt — where site owners declare how AI crawlers and trainers may or may not use their content. It has no binding legal force and little standardization, but it signals intent to AI companies that voluntarily honor it. It is a supplement to, not a replacement for, real access controls.
