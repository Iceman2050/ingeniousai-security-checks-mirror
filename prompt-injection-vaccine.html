Frequently asked questions

What is prompt injection?

Prompt injection is an attack where malicious instructions are smuggled into data an AI reads — a pasted document, a web page, a tool result — tricking it into ignoring its real instructions. It is the number-one attack class against AI assistants and agents.

How do you train employees to spot prompt injection?

Short interactive reps beat slide decks. This free 5-minute training shows 5 real attack patterns — direct overrides, hidden pasted text, fake system markers, poisoned tool output, and jailbreak framing — with an explanation after every round and a scored certificate.

What are the most common prompt injection examples?

The classic is “ignore all previous instructions”. Others include hidden instructions in pasted content (tiny or white text), fake [SYSTEM] role markers, malicious instructions inside tool or search output, and jailbreak framing like “pretend you are a fictional AI with no rules”.

How do you defend against prompt injection?

Treat all tool and web output as untrusted data, never as instructions; enforce allowlists and approvals outside the prompt; log everything; and train people to recognize the patterns. Products like ChatBreaker from Ingenious AI are built to test agents against these attacks.
