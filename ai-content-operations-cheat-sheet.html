Key facts

  • The brief is the quality lever: 5 parts — audience, voice, structure, facts, call to action — decide whether output reads as expertise or ai slop.
  • Facts are a section, not a hope: briefs that omit verifiable facts are exactly where invented statistics and quotes enter.
  • Disclosure is a workflow step: AI content must be labelled at first exposure and logged, the same way every time — not remembered per post.

What makes AI content read as ai slop?

AI slop is what a model produces with no brief: generic structure, stock phrases, invented-sounding claims and a voice that could belong to anyone. Readers and search engines both discount it. The cause is rarely the model — every team has the same models now — it is the missing operating system around the model: no named audience, no voice rules, no facts, no review.

What is the 5-part content brief?

  • 1. Audience — who is this for, specifically? Skeptical and short on time, or curious and deep-reading? Name them.
  • 2. Voice — direct and plain-spoken, with a never-use list: 'delve', 'tapestry', 'game-changer', 'in today's fast-paced world'. If a sentence could sit on anyone's blog, rewrite it.
  • 3. Structure — a hook in the first 2 lines, sections that each answer one question, a one-line takeaway per section, and a defined length.
  • 4. Facts — only claims you can verify, listed in the brief. Never invent statistics, quotes or case studies; an empty facts section is where hallucinations get in.
  • 5. Call to action — exactly one. No 'in conclusion', no summary paragraph that repeats the piece back to the reader.

What does the 6-stage workflow look like?

  1. Brief — the 5-part brief is filled before any prompt is written.
  2. Draft — the model drafts against the brief; structure and facts come from the brief, not from improvisation.
  3. Review — a human checks facts, voice and the never-use list; AI-written does not mean human-approved by default.
  4. Disclose — the AI-generated label goes on the content itself at first exposure, with machine-readable provenance where the format supports it.
  5. Publish — one call to action, metadata and canonical set deliberately, not by template accident.
  6. Record — a dated log of the asset, its label and its version, so disclosure can be proven later.

Frequently asked questions

What is ai slop?

AI slop is low-effort AI-generated content: generic structure, stock phrases, unverifiable claims and a voice that belongs to no one. It is produced by prompting without an operating system — no brief, no facts, no review. Content operations — a sharp brief, human review, disclosure and records — is how teams publish AI content that is not slop.

How do you make AI content sound human?

Start before the prompt: name a specific audience, write voice rules with a never-use list, fix the structure, and supply only verifiable facts in the brief. Then review like an editor — checking facts and cutting any sentence that could appear on anyone's blog. The model supplies speed; the workflow supplies the judgment.

Do I have to disclose AI-generated content?

In many contexts, yes. The EU AI Act's Article 50 duties require deployers to disclose AI-generated content at first exposure, and ad and social platforms run their own labelling rules. The safe operating pattern is a visible label on the content itself plus a dated record of every labelled asset.
