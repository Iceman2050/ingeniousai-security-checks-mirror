Cybersecurity News Today: Railway Breach Exposed Stored API Keys, and Rotation Is the Fix

The urgent story in cybersecurity news today is a break-in at Railway, the platform many teams use to host apps and databases. Railway published a preliminary report on October 22, 2026, written by Jacob Cooper and Angelo Saraceno. It says an attacker was inside Railway's internal systems from October 20 to October 22, 2026, hunting AI provider API keys, and decrypted a subset of the environment variables customers store on the platform. At most 1.85% of all workspaces, about 100,000 workspaces, had some variables potentially compromised. If you signed up for Railway before October 21 and stored keys there, Railway's advice is plain: rotate them. The checker below scores your rotation risk using Railway's own risk buckets.

Honest scope, read this first: Railway cannot tell which specific variables in a project were decrypted, because the stored keys were envelope encrypted and the query the attacker ran cannot be tied to individual keys. That unknown is the whole story: the advice is rotate by default, not rotate if you get a definite-impact email. Three more limits from the report itself. The attacker did not access user accounts. Railway has not ruled out that the attacker accessed user source code; the final forensic report will confirm. And a vendor revocation email is not proof of compromise: Railway gave some vendors every key stored on the platform, so notices went to more users than were impacted.

What happened, per Railway's preliminary report

  • The window: Railway believes the attack ran from October 20, 13:58 UTC to October 22, 03:32 UTC. The end time is when Railway confirmed the attacker no longer had access to any internal system.
  • Detection: monitoring paged an on-call engineer over abnormal latency on October 20. Railway identified a privilege escalation in its build infrastructure, halted build pipelines, and at 19:53 UTC on October 20 became aware it was facing a sustained and targeted campaign.
  • The goal: AI provider API keys, to perform distillation attacks. Railway says the attacker had access to abliterated models and substantial compute, which enabled highly sophisticated exploits. Railway has not published the exploit details or indicators of compromise; forensics are ongoing and a final report is promised.
  • The path in: the attacker first pulled secrets in encrypted form, then reached credentials for Railway's CI environment, and used compromised credentials to decrypt a subset of the environment variables it had queried from Railway's database.
  • Customer impact during the fight: new builds and deployments were paused and the dashboard, API, CLI and support tooling had periods of unavailability. Running deployments stayed online throughout. Builds for all tiers are now restored.

The scale needs care. Railway says at most 1.85% of all workspaces had some environment variables potentially compromised, which it puts at about 100,000 workspaces. It also says the number of users touched by vendor revocation requests is larger than 100,000, because out of caution it handed some vendors all of the keys stored on the platform. Railway names two high-risk buckets it is contacting proactively: workspaces with a very large number of environment variables, and environments with a high amount of AI keys from vendors. If your workspace sits in either bucket, treat this as a rotate-today item even if no email has arrived.

Why this cybersecurity news today story is about keys, not accounts

Most breach advice starts with passwords. This one does not, because the attacker never needed yours. Railway states plainly that the attacker did not access Railway user accounts and that individual accounts were not logged into. The break-in hit internal systems, and the prize was the secrets layer underneath the accounts: the API keys, database credentials and registry credentials that apps store as environment variables so they can run unattended. Whoever holds those keys can call the provider as your app, spend against your account, and read what your app can read, all without touching your login.

That is also why the vendor list matters. On October 21 at 09:25 UTC, Railway proactively notified vendors and asked them to revoke keys. The report names GitHub, GitLab, Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter, Stripe, and Google among them. If you received a revocation notice from one of those vendors this week, this incident is the likely reason. Railway's own response went further than notifications: it rolled all of its internal keys, then rolled them again after confirming the attacker was out; it rolled credentials for all storage buckets on the platform; it rebuilt its internal tooling from validated source; it accelerated a move to fourth-generation single-tenant builder infrastructure; it engaged external incident response experts and the authorities; and it added a way to mass-revoke Railway application permissions whenever suspicious activity is suspected.

Run the Railway rotation check now

  • List what you stored: every environment variable in every Railway project and environment. You cannot rotate what you have not listed, and Railway cannot tell you which of your variables were decrypted.
  • Rotate AI provider keys first: they are what the attacker came for. Then database credentials, then payment keys such as Stripe, then everything else, including private registry credentials.
  • Use the safe order for every key: create the new key at the provider, update it in Railway, redeploy the service so the new key is live, then revoke the old key at the provider. A key that was only changed inside Railway can still work for whoever copied the old value.
  • Remove public network access to databases: Railway lists this as a customer action. A database that the public internet can reach turns one copied credential into a direct login.
  • Check the logs Railway names: your GitHub account audit logs and app access, and the audit logs in registries such as Docker Hub. Add each AI provider's usage logs for October 20 to 22, 2026 and look for calls your app did not make.
  • Replace what vendors revoked: Railway revoked all Railway API tokens and rolled all storage bucket credentials on its side, and vendors revoked keys at Railway's request. Anything revoked is dead: create replacements and update CI, scripts and GitHub Actions secrets that used the old values.
  • Ask Railway if you are unsure: support@railway.com. Railway says it is contacting definitely affected and high-risk customers directly, and a follow-up report is promised when the forensic investigation concludes.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Railway breach, in plain terms?

The Railway breach is a break-in at Railway, the app hosting platform, in which an attacker got into internal systems between October 20 and October 22, 2026 and decrypted a subset of the environment variables customers stored there. Railway published the details in a preliminary report on October 22, 2026. The attacker was hunting AI provider API keys. User accounts were not accessed.

How many workspaces were affected by the Railway breach?

Railway says at most 1.85% of all workspaces had some environment variables potentially compromised, about 100,000 workspaces. Because the keys were envelope encrypted, Railway cannot tell which specific variables in a project were decrypted. Railway is contacting customers it counts as definitely affected or high risk, including workspaces holding very many variables or many AI keys.

What was the attacker after in the Railway breach?

The attacker's goal, per Railway's report, was AI provider API keys, taken to perform distillation attacks. Railway says the attacker had access to abliterated models and substantial compute and used sophisticated exploits against its build infrastructure. Railway has not published the exploit details or indicators of compromise, and says forensic work is still running, with a final report to come.

A vendor emailed me that a key was revoked. Does that mean I was hacked?

No. On October 21, 2026, Railway asked vendors including GitHub, GitLab, Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter, Stripe and Google to revoke keys, and out of caution it gave some vendors every key stored on the platform. More users got revocation notices than were impacted, Railway says. Treat the email as a prompt to rotate, not as proof your workspace was compromised.

What should I do if I stored keys on Railway?

Rotate them. Railway recommends that everyone who signed up prior to October 21, 2026 rotate the keys stored on Railway, out of caution. Create the new key at the provider, update it in Railway, redeploy, then revoke the old key at the provider. Also remove public network access to databases, check GitHub audit logs and app access, and check Docker Hub registry audit logs.
