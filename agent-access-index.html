What does the agent-blocking wave mean for AI agents 2026?

It means the open-web assumption behind most AI agents 2026 roadmaps is breaking. Agents were built on the idea that the whole web is browsable; October 2026 proved that banks, airlines, and review platforms will close the door when liability is unclear. JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon warned on October 13 about "liability transfer" — who pays when an agent books, buys, or moves money wrongly. A Charles Schwab executive put the control problem bluntly: "the client cannot control the agent and the broker cannot control it either."

For site owners, the strategic question is no longer whether agents are coming, but on what terms: block them, charge them, or sign them as a sales channel. Every bank on the block list — JPMorgan Chase, Charles Schwab, Citibank, and Bank of America — announced after Dimon's October 13 warning: 100% of the banking wave. Retailers chose the third option. Banks chose the first. Most sites have not chosen at all — which is itself a policy, and usually the riskiest one.

What is the difference between 'blocks' and 'paid tier'?

Blocks means the site refuses agent traffic outright. Paid tier — Yelp's model — means agent access exists but must be purchased. A paid tier is a business model; a block is a closed door.
