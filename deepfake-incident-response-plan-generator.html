Cybersecurity FAQ

What should a deepfake incident response plan include?

Five parts: detection and triage within the first hour, containment through platform takedown requests and evidence preservation, pre-written communications including a holding statement, clear escalation roles with timelines, and a post-mortem to close gaps.

How fast must platforms remove deepfake content?

Under the US TAKE IT DOWN Act, covered platforms must remove flagged intimate deepfakes as soon as possible and no later than 48 hours after a valid notice. The FTC began enforcing these platform requirements on May 19, 2026, with fines up to about $53,000 per violation.

Should a company respond publicly to a deepfake?

Only if the deepfake has reached public visibility. A pre-written holding statement lets you respond fast without amplifying the content — never link to or repost the fake. If it is contained to a few inboxes, quiet takedowns beat a public announcement.

What is the most important action in the first hour of a deepfake incident?

Preserve evidence and file takedown requests in parallel. Screenshot every instance with URLs and timestamps before content disappears, and submit platform removal requests immediately — the 48-hour clock under the TAKE IT DOWN Act starts when the platform receives your valid notice.
