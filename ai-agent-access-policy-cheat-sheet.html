Key facts

  • 3 declarations do the work: who may access, what they may do, and the commercial terms that apply.
  • A policy is only real if it validates: malformed JSON at the right URL is a locked door with no keyhole — agents skip it and guess.

What does an agent access policy declare about ai governance?

An agent access policy declares the rules of your site in a form machines can apply without a human reading a terms page: which identified agents are welcome, which actions they may take — read, summarise, transact — and the commercial terms attached to each, such as attribution, rate limits or paid tiers. It turns your AI governance from a paragraph nobody's agent reads into a file every agent can.

  • Who: named agents, classes of agents, or verified identities — allow, deny or conditional per identity.
  • What: the actions permitted per identity — browsing, indexing, summarising, quoting, purchasing, or none.
  • Terms: attribution requirements, rate limits, caching rules and commercial terms for use beyond the free grant.
  • Contact and precedence: where disputes go, and how this file relates to robots.txt and your human terms.

How do you publish and validate one?

  1. Decide the defaults: what may an unlisted agent do? Deny-by-default and allow-by-default are both legitimate — ambiguity is not.
  2. Generate the file from the decisions, in the standard schema, rather than hand-writing JSON.
  3. Publish at /.well-known/agent-policy.json over HTTPS, served as JSON, with no login wall in front of it.
  4. Validate the live file: syntax, schema and reachability — the copy on the server, not the draft on your disk.
  5. Align robots.txt and terms so the three signals never contradict each other.
  6. Review on a cadence: new agents and new business terms both make last quarter's policy stale.

Frequently asked questions

What is an AI agent access policy?

It is a machine-readable file, published at /.well-known/agent-policy.json, that declares which AI agents may use your site, what actions each may take, and on what commercial terms. Unlike a human terms page, agents and tools can read and apply it automatically, which makes it an AI governance control rather than a statement of intent.

How is an agent policy different from robots.txt?

Robots.txt is a coarse, decades-old convention: mostly crawl permission per user-agent, with no actions, no commercial terms and no identity verification. An agent access policy is richer — per-agent permissions for specific actions like summarising or transacting, plus terms — and the two are designed to agree, with robots.txt handling classic crawling.

Where does the agent policy file live?

At the standard well-known location: https://your-domain/.well-known/agent-policy.json, served over HTTPS as JSON without authentication. The fixed location is the point — agents, validators and audit tools all look in exactly one place, the same way they do for security.txt and robots.txt.
