CVE patch-speed leaderboard: week of October 12–18, 2026

Eight vendors, one question: how many days did attackers get? This week's board covers the October CVE pileup — four same-day patches, one incomplete patch, one 12-day zero-day, and one flaw that was open for two years. Updated every week; every date sourced. The short version: For the week of October 12–18, 2026, Cisco, Palo Alto, and GitLab all patched the same day they disclosed (0 days each). AhsayCBS needed 7 days — after its August patches turned out incomplete. Fortinet took 12 days to ship fixed FortiMail builds for an actively exploited zero-day, and Zammad only half-fixed its chain. The full methodology and vendor correction process are below.

Methodology — exactly how "days to patch" is measured

  • Days to patch = patch release date minus first public disclosure date (vendor advisory, CVE publication, or researcher disclosure — whichever came first).
  • 0 = coordinated same-day patch + advisory (the standard mature vendors hit).
  • Tiebreak: pre-patch exploitation window — days attackers had before any fix existed.
  • Honest caveats: private notification dates are almost never public, so the clock starts at public disclosure. Exploitation-start dates are estimates from incident-response firms. This is the week's notable cases, not a census of the software industry.

What this week taught

Same-day is now the bar, not the exception. Three vendors hit 0 days this week — that's what mature patch operations look like. The real differentiators were elsewhere: incomplete patches (Ahsay's August "fix" that wasn't), no-patch-at-disclosure (Fortinet's 12-day zero-day, Zammad's LPE half), and unfixable branches (FortiMail 7.2). When you evaluate a vendor, ask three questions: do they patch the same day, is the patch actually complete, and does every supported branch get one?

What is a CVE checker?

A CVE checker is a free tool that tells you whether your software is affected by a published CVE — a publicly known cybersecurity vulnerability. It compares your version or configuration against the official advisory and gives you a patched, vulnerable, or unknown verdict in about 30 seconds.

How do I check if my software has a CVE?

Find your exact version number, then compare it against the vendor's security advisory or the NVD entry for the CVE. The fastest way is a CVE checker built for that product: paste your version and get an instant verdict against the published version tables.

How do I know if I am affected by a CVE?

You are affected when you run a vulnerable version and the vulnerable component is reachable or enabled — for example, open sign-ups, an exposed management port, or a vulnerable app installed. A CVE checker gives you the version verdict in seconds; the advisory's conditions tell you whether that verdict means real exposure.
