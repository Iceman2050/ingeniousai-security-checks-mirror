Why does an agent payment need its own authorization record?

Because the usual evidence does not exist. A human checkout leaves a session, a clickstream and a cardholder at a screen. An agent checkout leaves an API call. When the purchase is disputed, the only thing that separates an authorized agent payment from a rogue one is a record made at approval time, not reconstructed afterwards.

Build approval evidence that survives a dispute

  1. Record at approval time: capture who approved, what amount, which payee, which agent and when, in one record created when the approval happens.
  2. Make it tamper-evident: hash-chain receipts so each links to the previous one. An edited record breaks the chain and announces itself.
  3. Bind scope tightly: an approval covers one payment or one narrow mandate, never a standing 'agents may spend' permission.
  4. Practice the export: produce the dispute pack for a real past transaction and time it. Agentic Receipts makes this a single export.
  5. Keep receipts with the money: store the evidence trail beside the accounting records, retained at least as long as the dispute window.

Frequently asked questions

What is an authorization receipt for an agent payment?

An authorization receipt is a record created at approval time that binds a human approver to a specific agent action: the amount, the payee, the agent's identity and the time. Agentic Receipts stores these as SHA-256 hash-chained records, so the trail is tamper-evident and exportable when a payment is disputed.

What is agentic commerce?

Agentic commerce is commerce where an AI agent discovers, compares and purchases on a person's behalf, rather than the person checking out themselves. It requires merchants to expose machine-readable product and checkout paths, and it creates a new evidence problem: proving a human authorized what the agent bought. US search demand for the term reached 5,600 searches a month in 2026, up 464% year over year (Ahrefs).

How is this different from Mastercard Verifiable Intent or AP2?

The network and protocol systems carry authorization inside their own rails: Verifiable Intent at Mastercard, signed mandates in Google's AP2. Agentic Receipts is the business-side record: receipts your company issues, holds and exports across any rail, agent or vendor, so your evidence does not depend on one network's system.

What happens in a dispute without receipts?

Without an approval record, a disputed agent payment is one person's word against another's, settled by whoever holds the money. Merchants lose chargebacks they should win; buyers cannot prove an agent exceeded its mandate. The receipt trail is what turns the argument into a lookup.

Are receipts legal proof?

Receipts are evidence, and evidence supports disputes; they are not a legal guarantee and not legal advice. A hash-chained, time-stamped approval record is substantially stronger than chat logs or memory in any dispute process. How a specific chargeback or claim resolves still depends on the rail, the contract and the jurisdiction.
