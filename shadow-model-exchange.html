Methodology

The published exchange will aggregate fully anonymous responses: no names, no emails, no company identifiers. Respondents report (1) which models their company publicly acknowledges and (2) which they actually run in production — including shadow use. Results are published only in aggregate once enough responses arrive; per-company data is never disclosed. The live survey is the Shadow Model Census.

Context: GoodFirms' AI-tool tracking of 14,022 active companies finds ChatGPT used by 11,359 companies, Claude by 10,103, and Gemini by 4,294 (visible adoption, not shadow use). Stack Overflow's 2026 developer survey found only 24% of companies have published an approved AI toolset or formal AI policy — the reason the gap exists at all.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Shadow Model Exchange?

The Shadow Model Exchange is a planned anonymous, aggregated view of which LLMs companies actually run in production versus what they publicly admit to — the gap known as shadow AI. It launches publicly once enough real, anonymous responses arrive.

Is the data on this page real?

No. Everything on this page is an illustrative sample, clearly badged as such, shown only to demonstrate the exchange's format. The published exchange will launch after enough real, anonymous responses come in through the Shadow Model Census.

How will the real exchange work?

Respond anonymously to the Shadow Model Census with the LLMs you run in production and the ones your company publicly acknowledges. Once enough responses arrive, we publish aggregated, anonymized deltas — no names, no emails, no company identifiers.

Why does the admitted-vs-actual gap matter?

Much enterprise AI use is shadow IT: Stack Overflow's 2026 developer survey found only 24% of companies have published an approved AI toolset or formal AI policy, so most model choices are invisible to security and compliance teams — creating unknown attack surface.
