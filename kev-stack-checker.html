Questions

What is CISA's KEV catalog?

The Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog is maintained by the U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. It lists only vulnerabilities confirmed to be actively exploited in the wild, with required-action deadlines for U.S. federal agencies. A match here means the flaw is not theoretical — attackers are using it.

How fresh is the data in this checker?

The checker embeds a dated snapshot of the CISA catalog, fetched on 2026-10-16. It is not live data. Always confirm findings against CISA's current catalog and the vendor's advisory before acting, especially for time-sensitive patching decisions.

Does my software list leave my computer?

No. The catalog snapshot downloads with this page and every comparison runs in JavaScript in your browser. No request is made with your input, nothing is logged, nothing leaves your machine.

Why do I have to verify my version manually?

The CISA catalog names vulnerable products but does not publish machine-readable affected-version ranges for every entry. The checker tells you which of your products have known exploited flaws; you then confirm your exact version against the linked vendor advisory.

What should I do if something matches?

Treat it as urgent: exploited vulnerabilities are the highest-priority patches in any stack. Check the vendor advisory for the fixed version, patch or mitigate, and — if you were exposed before patching — look for signs of compromise. The due dates shown are CISA's deadlines for federal agencies; fix sooner.

What is a CVE checker?

A CVE checker is a free tool that tells you whether your software is affected by a published CVE — a publicly known cybersecurity vulnerability. It compares your version or configuration against the official advisory and gives you a patched, vulnerable, or unknown verdict in about 30 seconds.

How do I check if my software has a CVE?

Find your exact version number, then compare it against the vendor's security advisory or the NVD entry for the CVE. The fastest way is a CVE checker built for that product: paste your version and get an instant verdict against the published version tables.

How do I know if I am affected by a CVE?

You are affected when you run a vulnerable version and the vulnerable component is reachable or enabled — for example, open sign-ups, an exposed management port, or a vulnerable app installed. A CVE checker gives you the version verdict in seconds; the advisory's conditions tell you whether that verdict means real exposure.
