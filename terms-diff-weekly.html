Terms Diff of the Week — Week 1

What actually changed in AI vendor terms this week. We fetch the real pages, we cite the real sources, and when we can't verify a change, we say so — an honest short edition beats a fabricated long one.

🔍 How we check

  • Pages we fetch every week: Anthropic's usage policy (anthropic.com/legal/aup), OpenAI's usage & ad policies, Google's Gemini Apps privacy hub, Meta's commercial terms, and xAI's terms of service — plus vendor changelogs where published.
  • Weekly cadence: every edition is dated. This one covers the check run on October 17, 2026.
  • What counts as a "change": a new or removed clause, a new effective date, or a revised policy version — verifiable on the vendor's own page or in credible reporting with a named source and date. Anything we can't verify lands under "no verifiable change." We never invent one.

✅ Two verified changes this week

Anthropic · checked Oct 17, 2026

Usage policy overhaul: new rules on model abuse, deception, weapons, surveillance VERIFIED CHANGE

Anthropic announced a 2026 usage policy update on October 8, 2026, effective November 12, 2026. The headline addition: a ban on "sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior toward our models" — a first-of-its-kind rule we verified as live on Anthropic's policy page today. The update also consolidates deceptive-campaign rules into one section, expands the weapons restriction to explicitly cover weaponizing drones and autonomous vehicles, clarifies surveillance restrictions, and requires a human in the loop plus AI-use labeling for consequential recommendations in legal, medical, finance, and other high-risk areas. Anthropic describes most of it as clarification of existing rules; the anti-abuse clause and the drone-weaponization language are the genuinely new parts.

Sources: TechCrunch, Oct 8, 2026 · Anthropic Usage Policy (live page, fetched Oct 17, 2026) · Anthropic's announcement

Meta · checked Oct 17, 2026

New commercial terms authorize scraping business websites, effective Oct 30 VERIFIED CHANGE

Meta published updated commercial terms taking effect October 30, 2026, that authorize Meta to use scrapers, bots, and crawlers to access, collect, and analyze content from websites "associated with" businesses using its platforms — and to use that content to train, develop, and improve its products, including AI. The changes were first reported by Nieman Lab and covered by The Verge on October 16, 2026. Meta says the update consolidates existing permissions and doesn't change how it currently crawls; businesses that disagree have no opt-out — the stated alternative is to stop using Meta's products for business. If you run a website linked to a Meta business presence, the clause that binds you is the terms text, not the reassurance.

Sources: tech-insider.org (citing The Verge, Nieman Lab, Oct 16, 2026) · Latestly (Oct 16–17, 2026 coverage)
