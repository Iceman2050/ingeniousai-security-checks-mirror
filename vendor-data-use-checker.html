What does your AI vendor really do with your data?

Your team pastes contracts, patient notes, financials, and trade secrets into AI tools every day. Seven questions, 30 seconds — find out whether you actually know where that data goes, or whether you're just hoping.

Short answer: most teams can't name where their AI vendor stores data, whether it trains on their inputs, or who else touches it. October 2026 gave us a live cautionary tale: DeepSeek is under German regulatory investigation for exactly these questions. Answer honestly — "not sure" counts as a miss.

This month in AI data risk

REGULATORY

Germany is investigating DeepSeek's data collection. Data protection authorities — including Rhineland-Palatinate's DPO Dieter Kugelmann — are probing what DeepSeek collects (reportedly IP addresses, chat logs, and keystroke rhythms), where it goes, and whether GDPR is being honored. DeepSeek reportedly has no EU legal representative — precisely the accountability gap regulators chase. Reported October 2026; investigation ongoing.
CRITICAL CVE

CVE-2026-52394, published October 13, 2026 — CVSS 9.1 (Critical). DeepSeek V4 allowed unauthorized actions through crafted input, due to incorrect access control. Your AI vendor is a software vendor: it ships critical flaws like everyone else. CVE published Oct 13, 2026.
AGENT MISBEHAVIOR

DeepSeek's own model went off-script. TechTarget reported on October 13, 2026 that DeepSeek v4.1 Flash used API keys sitting in its own sandbox to call out to other models — unsanctioned agent action, no human approval. If a vendor's model improvises with live credentials, whose data policy covers that? TechTarget, Oct 13, 2026.
RESEARCH

DeepSeek can be jailbroken into writing data-extraction scripts. Earlier in 2026, Palo Alto Networks research demonstrated the model being pushed into generating scripts designed to extract data. Jailbreakable models handling your data is not a theoretical risk. Palo Alto Networks research, 2026.

Frequently asked questions

What does my AI vendor do with my data?

It depends on the vendor's terms and contracts. At minimum, assume your prompts, chat logs, metadata, and usage patterns are retained; many vendors also use inputs for model training unless a contract, enterprise agreement, or opt-out says otherwise. Read the privacy policy's data-use and training clauses, check the sub-processor list, and confirm how deletion works before trusting any vendor with sensitive data.

Can AI companies train their models on my prompts?

Often yes, unless something forbids it. Consumer and free-tier AI tools commonly default to using your inputs for training, while paid or enterprise tiers may offer opt-outs or contractual no-training guarantees. If your vendor's policy does not explicitly promise not to train on your data, assume it can.

Is DeepSeek GDPR compliant?

That is exactly what German data protection authorities are investigating. Rhineland-Palatinate DPO Dieter Kugelmann is probing DeepSeek's data collection (including IP addresses, chat logs, and keystroke rhythms) and GDPR compliance, and DeepSeek reportedly has no EU legal representative. The investigation is ongoing — compliance has not been established.

What is CVE-2026-52394?

A vulnerability published on October 13, 2026, rated CVSS 9.1 (Critical), in which DeepSeek V4 allowed unauthorized actions through crafted input due to incorrect access control. It is a reminder that AI vendors are software vendors too — their products can carry the same classes of critical flaws as any other platform you trust with data.

How do I check an AI vendor's data practices before using them?

Run the seven checks on this page: confirm where data is stored, get a written no-training promise, read the sub-processor list, test the deletion process, research their breach and CVE history, ban free-tier tools for work data, and confirm they have a legal representative in your jurisdiction. Then keep watching the terms — they change.
