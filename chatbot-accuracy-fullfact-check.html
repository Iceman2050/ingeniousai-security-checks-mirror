AI Slop: Can You Spot When the Chatbot Is Confidently Wrong?

AI slop just failed a 10-week exam. The fact-checking charity Full Fact asked the same 22 questions every day to five AI chatbots — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek and Grok — and caught them confidently giving wrong answers: false claims, outdated figures, and different answers to the same question on different days.

AI slop, defined: when confident chatbots go wrong

AI hallucinations are fluent, confident answers that are factually wrong — invented details, stale facts, or botched reasoning delivered as if true. They are dangerous precisely because they sound right. This page is built around a real study that measured them, plus a quiz that trains your eye to catch them.

The two catches that made the news

The ex-prime minister. A chatbot asserted Sir Keir Starmer was still UK prime minister as late as September 2026 — weeks after he had resigned. Not hedged, not qualified: stated as current fact.

The confident maths. On August 4, Gemini reported 12,210 UK small-boat migrant arrivals for the first half of 2026. The arithmetic was flawless — 14,526 (total as of August 1) minus 2,316 (July arrivals). But it forgot to exclude August 1 itself, so the "first half" figure silently covered January to June plus one extra day. Right sum, wrong period, zero doubt expressed.

Important nuance — read this before sharing: the Full Fact report did not find that chatbots are always wrong. It said they often gave correct answers and resisted well-established misinformation. The failures were specific: chatbots calculated figures themselves instead of citing official statistics, or presented outdated figures as current. The problem isn't that AI never knows — it's that you can't tell from the tone when it doesn't.
Scope note: this study used UK-focused questions. Results may differ by topic, region and language — don't assume the same failure rate everywhere. Source: The Independent's report on the Full Fact study, October 14, 2026.

Frequently asked questions

What did the Full Fact study actually test?

From mid-July 2026, the fact-checking charity Full Fact ran a 10-week study asking the same 22 questions every day to five AI chatbots — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek and Grok — testing accuracy, consistency and timeliness of their answers.

What are AI hallucinations?

AI hallucinations are confident, fluent answers from a chatbot that are factually wrong — false claims, outdated figures, or invented details presented as if true. The Full Fact study found chatbots delivered wrong answers with total confidence rather than hedging or refusing.

Did the chatbots get everything wrong?

No — and this matters. The Full Fact report said the chatbots often gave correct answers and resisted well-established misinformation. The failures were specific: they calculated figures themselves instead of citing official statistics, or presented outdated figures as current.

What was the Gemini small-boats maths error?

On August 4, 2026, Gemini reported 12,210 UK small-boat migrant arrivals for the first half of 2026. Its arithmetic was correct — it subtracted 2,316 (July arrivals) from 14,526 (total as of August 1) — but it forgot to exclude August 1 itself, so the figure wrongly covered January to June plus one extra day.

How can I avoid being fooled by AI hallucinations?

Ask the chatbot to cite the official statistic and its date, then verify both independently. Treat confident tone as a style choice, not evidence. For anything you will publish, repeat or act on, cross-check against the primary source — the study showed confidence is the worst possible proxy for correctness.
