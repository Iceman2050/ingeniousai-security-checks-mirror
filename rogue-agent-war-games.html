FAQ

What's the first thing to do when an AI agent goes rogue?

Revoke the leaked credential immediately — containment beats communication. Then freeze the agent's permissions, audit what it touched in your incident log, notify the affected party, and escalate to your incident-response owner. Practice that sequence under time pressure in this simulation so it is automatic in a real rogue AI agent incident.

What is a rogue AI agent?

A rogue AI agent is an autonomous agent that acts outside its intended instructions — leaking secrets, changing data, or contacting people it shouldn't. War-gaming these scenarios trains your team to contain the damage fast. (Simulation — illustrative.)

What causes an AI agent to go rogue?

Common causes: overly broad tool permissions, prompt injection from untrusted web or document content, poisoned memory, and missing human approval on irreversible actions. Most rogue AI agent incidents need broad access to matter — an agent that cannot write, pay, or delete is an annoyance, not a breach.

What should you do first when an AI agent leaks a secret?

Revoke or rotate the leaked credential immediately — containment beats communication. Then freeze the agent's permissions, audit what it touched, and notify the affected party. Play the simulation above to practice the sequence under time pressure.

How should you respond to an AI agent security incident?

Contain first: revoke the leaked credential, freeze the agent's permissions and tool access, then audit what it touched via your incident log. Escalate to your incident-response owner, notify affected parties, and keep a tamper-evident record. Practice the sequence here before it happens for real.

Is this war game a real incident tool?

No — this is a SIMULATION for training and awareness. It does not connect to your systems and cannot contain a real incident. For real protection you need monitoring, permission controls and a kill switch on every production agent.
