Key facts

  • The ladder has 3 rungs: +1 day, +7 days and +14 days past due — each with a defined tone and a defined ask.
  • Nothing sends itself: every message is drafted, then approved by a human who sees the exact text — approval of that message, not of the idea of messages.
  • Escalation is not aggression: the ladder firms up facts and consequences while keeping the door open — most late payers are disorganised, not dishonest.

What is the agentic ai late-payment escalation ladder?

The ladder is a fixed sequence of reminders keyed to days past due, so no invoice drifts into silence and no chase depends on someone's mood. The +1 message assumes an oversight and makes paying effortless. The +7 message restates the facts and asks for a date. The +14 message names the consequences and the final date — politely, in writing, with the relationship still intact.

  • +1 day — the nudge: friendly, assumes an oversight, restates the amount and due date, attaches the invoice again, one-click payment route.
  • +7 days — the restatement: firmer; references the first reminder, itemises what is owed, asks for payment or a committed date by return.
  • +14 days — the consequence: states what happens next — work paused, late terms applied, escalation to a formal demand — with a final clear date.
  • After the ladder: stop conditions take over — a promise logged, a dispute raised, or a payment plan agreed each pause the sequence deliberately, never accidentally.

What are the drafting and approval rules?

  • Draft from the record: invoice number, amount, due date and work delivered — never from memory or vibes.
  • One ask per message: pay, or reply with a date. Two asks produce neither.
  • Human approval per message: the approver sees the exact text, recipient and amount — that specific send is what gets approved.
  • Log everything: sends, replies, promises and pauses form the aged-receivables record you would want if the matter ever leaves email.
  • Never auto-send in anger: tone is set by the rung, not by the balance or the day someone is having.

When should you send the first late-payment reminder?

One day past due. The +1 reminder assumes an oversight — invoices get buried, approvers travel — and makes paying effortless by re-attaching the invoice and the payment route. Teams that wait a polite month train customers that terms are decorative; the ladder keeps terms factual without starting a fight.

How firm should late-payment reminders get?

Firmer in facts, never in temper. The +7 restates what is owed and asks for a date; the +14 names real consequences — paused work, late terms, a formal demand — with a final date. Each rung stays polite and specific, because most late payers are disorganised rather than dishonest, and today's chased invoice is next quarter's customer.
