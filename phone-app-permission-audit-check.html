PHONE HYGIENE — 5-MINUTE AUDIT

Your phone isn't hacked. It's just oversharing.

A viral post says "delete these apps tonight" — free VPNs with no website, flashlight apps holding your contacts, abandoned apps, sideloaded keyboards. The fear is overblown in places, but the underlying advice is sound: most phones leak through permissions you granted years ago and forgot. Five questions, five minutes, honest answers — including where the viral claims go too far.

Short answer: on modern phones, no app silently steals your address book — you handed over the permissions. The fix isn't panic-deleting; it's a permission audit: check what each app can access, delete what you don't use, and kill the free VPN from a developer with no website. Do the five below.

The 5-minute audit — do it now

1. Review permissions (the highest-leverage 2 minutes)

Android: Settings → Privacy → Permission Manager — tap Contacts, Microphone, Location, Camera and revoke anything that doesn't need it. iPhone: Settings → Privacy & Security — same review. A flashlight with your contacts is a revoke, not a scandal.

2. Delete the free VPN with no website

A VPN sees all your traffic. Free VPNs from anonymous developers have been repeatedly caught logging and selling user data. Keep a reputable one or none.

3. Delete apps you haven't opened in 6+ months

Abandoned apps don't get security patches, and ownership can change hands — a new owner can push an update that adds data collection you never agreed to.

4. Kill the sideloaded keyboard

A keyboard sees every password you type. Anything installed from outside the Play Store / App Store skipped review entirely. Go back to the default.

5. Check the "free" utilities

QR scanners, cleaners, photo editors, song identifiers — if it's free, ask what it collects. Check its permissions; check whether the developer has a real website and privacy policy.

Where the viral post overreaches (honest corrections)

  • "A flashlight is building a copy of your entire address book." — Only if you granted it contacts access. Modern OSes require explicit permission grants. The danger is real but it's your forgotten grants, not silent theft.
  • "My cousin in cybersecurity said…" — Unverifiable authority. The advice stands on its own merits; it doesn't need an anonymous cousin.
  • What it gets right: free VPNs from no-name developers, sideloaded keyboards, and abandoned apps are genuinely among the highest-risk things on a phone. The permission-manager check is the single best habit in the list.
