    Frequently asked questions

    How much does AI insurance cost?

    There is no public rate card for AI agent liability insurance. Standalone capacity exists — Armilla AI, a Lloyd's coverholder, raised its AI liability limits to $25M per organization in January 2026 — but premiums are underwritten per deployment. This calculator gives an illustrative range from a fixed scoring table: your agent's tool permissions drive a risk score, and the score maps to a band. It is educational, not a quote.

    What does AI liability insurance cover?

    Standalone AI liability policies, such as Armilla AI's, are built for how AI systems actually fail: AI model errors, harmful or misleading outputs, AI agent failures including incorrect decisions and improper tool use, data leakage via outputs, AI-driven property damage, and defense costs for AI regulatory investigations such as under the EU AI Act.

    Do I need insurance for my AI agent?

    If your agent takes real-world actions — sending email, moving money, writing code or databases — you carry real-world liability. Since January 1, 2026, ISO's generative AI exclusion endorsements (CG 40 47, CG 40 48, CG 35 08) let carriers remove AI-related claims from standard commercial general liability policies, so your existing policy may no longer respond to agent-caused losses. Check your renewal paperwork.

    What are ISO's generative AI exclusions?

    In January 2026, ISO (Verisk's standard-forms division) introduced three optional endorsements for commercial general liability policies: CG 40 47 (broad generative AI exclusion), CG 40 48 (advertising injury only), and CG 35 08 (products/completed operations). When attached, they remove coverage for claims arising from generative AI — closing the old "silent AI" gap where coverage was ambiguous.

    How is the illustrative premium range calculated?

    Each tool permission carries a fixed weight (code execution and payments highest, read-only access lowest), plus a surcharge if no human approves irreversible actions. The total becomes a 0–100 risk score and a grade A–F. The illustrative annual premium is the score times $120, banded plus or minus 25%. The table is fixed and transparent — not actuarial, not a quote.
