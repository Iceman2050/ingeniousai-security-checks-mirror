The Data-Flow Mapper: where do your prompts go after you hit send?

You type into a chatbot. The answer comes back. But what happened to your words in between? ⚠ The verified facts of the probe (as reported)

  • Anthropic's allegation: a 154-page threat-intelligence report published September 10, 2026 claimed DeepSeek routed 12.1 million user queries to Claude over a 14-day window in July, and that Moonshot AI routed 23 million+ exchanges May–July plus ~300,000 customer requests over 10 days through 5,380 accounts.
  • The sensitive slice: Anthropic alleged some forwarded material was sensitive — including data tied to a municipal Public Security Bureau's case-management system and surveillance footage — and said it could not confirm whether Moonshot told customers their requests were being sent to a third party.
  • The regulator: China's Cyberspace Administration (CAC) reportedly summoned representatives from seven AI companies named in the report — DeepSeek, Moonshot, Alibaba, Zhipu, SenseTime, MiniMax, Xiaomi — then narrowed its investigation to DeepSeek and Moonshot, reportedly examining whether sensitive police, military and state-linked data crossed borders to a U.S. AI system.
  • Status: investigation ongoing; outcome unknown. Allegations are allegations — not findings.

Frequently asked questions

Did DeepSeek send user data to Claude?

Anthropic alleged it: in a 154-page threat-intelligence report published September 10, 2026, Anthropic claimed DeepSeek routed 12.1 million user queries to Claude over a 14-day window in July. China's regulator (the CAC) is now investigating those allegations. Nothing has been proven in court or by an independent audit — treat the claims as alleged, not as established fact.

Why is China investigating DeepSeek and Moonshot AI?

After Anthropic's September 10, 2026 report named seven Chinese AI companies as unauthorized Claude users, the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC) summoned representatives from all seven — DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, Zhipu, SenseTime, MiniMax and Xiaomi — and narrowed its probe to DeepSeek and Moonshot. Regulators are reportedly examining whether sensitive police, military and state-linked data crossed China's borders to a U.S. AI system without users' knowledge.

Can an AI company forward my prompts to a third party without telling me?

The allegation is exactly that: Anthropic said DeepSeek relayed selected user requests to Claude while users believed they were dealing with DeepSeek's own models, and said it could not confirm whether Moonshot told customers their requests were being sent to a third party. Whether a vendor may do this legally depends on its terms of service and the data-protection law of the jurisdiction involved — which is why reading the terms and the data-routing clauses matters.

Do AI chatbots use my prompts to train their models?

It depends on the vendor and the plan. Many consumer AI chatbots use conversations for training by default unless you opt out or use a business/API tier with a no-training commitment. Anthropic separately alleged that DeepSeek used Claude's outputs — including hidden reasoning steps it calls the "thinking signature" — to train its own systems, a practice called illicit distillation. Check your vendor's policy for the exact rule that applies to you.

How can I reduce the risk of my prompt data being misused?

Five practical moves: use a business or API tier with a contractual no-training commitment; never paste sensitive personal, client, or credentials-bearing data into consumer chatbots; read the vendor's terms on data retention, third-party sharing and sub-processors; check which jurisdiction your data is processed in; and re-check the terms periodically — vendors can and do change data-routing practices.
