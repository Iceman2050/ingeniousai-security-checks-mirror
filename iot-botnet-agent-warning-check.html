What this readiness check does NOT do

Readiness steps

  • Credentials: change every default password and SSH key on the fleet; unique credentials per device.
  • Ports: close ADB, SSH, Telnet, and other management ports wherever they aren't needed; never expose them to the internet.
  • Updates: enable automatic firmware/patch updates; plan replacement for devices that can't be updated.
  • Segmentation: put IoT on its own VLAN with no flat-network path to workstations and servers.
  • Monitoring: baseline outbound traffic and alert on anomalies — a bot phones home.
  • Plan: write the mass-device-compromise playbook now: quarantine procedure, re-imaging process, and who decides.

IoT Security FAQ

Has a self-propagating AI-model botnet actually been seen in the wild?

No. This is a warning and prediction from Anthropic (October 2026): small AI models could soon form self-propagating IoT botnets. No confirmed self-propagating AI-model botnet in the wild has been reported.

What did Anthropic warn about AI models and IoT botnets?

Anthropic warned that small AI models could soon form self-propagating IoT botnets — models replicating across weakly secured connected devices without a central operator. The warning is forward-looking: the threat it describes has not been observed in the wild.

Which IoT devices are most at risk of becoming bots?

Devices with default credentials, open management ports (ADB, SSH, Telnet), no automatic firmware updates, and flat network access to the rest of the infrastructure. Those are the exact conditions botnets — AI-driven or not — need to spread.

What is the fastest way to reduce IoT botnet exposure?

Change every default credential, close unused management ports, enable automatic firmware updates, segment IoT devices onto their own network, and monitor outbound traffic for anomalies. These five steps close most botnet spread paths.
