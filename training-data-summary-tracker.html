Half the AI industry hasn't published the EU AI Act training-data summary.

Since August 2025, EU law has required providers of general-purpose AI models to publish a summary of what they trained on — the EU AI Act training-data summary. It's the one document about these models the law makes public — so anyone can go and look for it. An independent audit did. Short answer: the publishing providers, per the audit's coding, are Anthropic, Cohere, DeepSeek, Microsoft, MiniMax, NVIDIA, OpenAI, Qwen, Tencent, Thinking Machines Lab, and Z.ai. The audit does not name which four publish only a related document or which six publish nothing — and nothing in the report is a finding of legal compliance or breach.

How the audit worked — and what it can't say

  • Method: one model per provider, coded from the provider's own documents under a protocol and codebook fixed (and hash-recorded) before collection. 13 providers were read twice, blind; inter-coder consistency 0.893 (Krippendorff's alpha). All 1,113 answers carry source addresses, reading dates, and quotations in the open evidence file.
  • Two codings, both printed: on the rule fixed before the study, 7 of 21 publish; on the final exploratory coding, 11 of 21. The researchers print both, because which documents count turns on a question that is still open.
  • Not a legal finding: the researchers state explicitly that nothing in the report is a finding that any provider — or the Commission — complied with or breached the law.
  • Models predating the duty: models placed on the market before 2 August 2025 have until 2 August 2027, which covers several of the studied models.

Frequently asked questions

What is the EU AI Act training-data summary?

Since 2 August 2025, providers of general-purpose AI models must publish a public summary of the content used to train them (Article 53 GPAI duties). Models placed on the market before that date have until 2 August 2027. The European Commission published a mandatory template for the summary on 24 July 2025. It is the one document about these models that the Act makes public by law — so anyone can go and look for it.

What did the October 16 preprint audit find?

The EU AI Act Transparency Audit (AI Tools Police, preprint published 16 October 2026) read the public websites of 26 AI model providers. Of the 21 whose summary deadline had passed for the model studied: 11 publish a summary that presents itself as such and opens without an account (Anthropic, Cohere, DeepSeek, Microsoft, MiniMax, NVIDIA, OpenAI, Qwen, Tencent, Thinking Machines Lab, Z.ai); 4 publish only a related document such as a model card; and for 6 the researchers found nothing in the places they looked.

Does a missing summary mean a provider broke the law?

Not by itself. The researchers state explicitly that nothing in the report is a finding that any provider complied with or breached the law. The study checked specific public pages on specific dates, and models placed on the market before 2 August 2025 have until 2 August 2027 to publish. A missing summary is a transparency gap, not a legal verdict.

What counts as a summary that presents itself as such?

Under the study's coding, a document the provider itself titles as the training-data summary, which names the model it covers and opens without an account. Of the 11 summaries found, 9 name the model studied; Z.ai's names the GLM-5 family without naming the model studied, and Qwen's describes the models behind its platform without naming one.

What about copyright policies and opt-outs?

The Act does not require providers to publish their copyright policy, and the audit found 0 of 26 present one as such (2 of 26 state that one exists). But 10 of 26 publish a specific route by which a rightsholder can reserve their works against training use — such as the name of the crawler to block.
