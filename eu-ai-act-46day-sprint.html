Article 50 transparency obligations have applied since 2 August 2026 — but providers of generative AI systems already on the market before that date get one grace period: until 2 December 2026 to put machine-readable marking on synthetic outputs (Art. 50(2)). Frequently asked questions

What is EU AI Act Article 50?

Article 50 of the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) sets transparency obligations: providers of direct-interaction systems must inform users they're interacting with AI (50(1)); providers of synthetic content generators must mark outputs machine-readable (50(2)); deployers of emotion-recognition/biometric systems must inform exposed persons (50(3)); deployers must disclose deepfakes and AI-generated public-interest text (50(4)). Applied since 2 August 2026.

What happens on 2 December 2026 for Article 50?

It's the end of the narrow grace period for Article 50(2) machine-readable marking: providers of generative AI systems already on the market before 2 August 2026 must have marking in place by then. Newer systems and all other Article 50 duties have applied since 2 August 2026 with no grace period.

Am I a provider or a deployer under the EU AI Act?

Roughly: the provider develops an AI system and places it on the market or puts it into service under its own name (Art. 3(3)); the deployer uses an AI system under its authority (Art. 3(4)). A creator calling a generation API is generally the deployer, not the provider. Your role decides which Article 50 limb applies.

What are the fines for violating Article 50?

Under Article 99(4): up to €15 million or 3% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. SMEs and start-ups pay the lower of the two. That's not the top tier — Article 5 prohibited-practice violations carry up to €35M or 7%.

Is this checklist legal advice?

No — automated guidance, not legal advice. Every item cites its Article 50 limb, but confirm your obligations with qualified counsel before relying on them.
