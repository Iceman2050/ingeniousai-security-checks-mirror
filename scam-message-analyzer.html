What is phishing by text message?

Phishing by text message — also called smishing — is a scam where criminals send fake texts pretending to be your bank, a delivery company, or a government agency. The message creates urgency ("your account is locked", "you missed a delivery") to trick you into clicking a malicious link or replying with personal information.

    How the phishing scoring works

    Each red flag carries a weight. Known scam scripts from our curated blocklist (+30), government-agency impersonation (+25), legal threats like "arrest warrant" (+20), payment demands via gift cards or crypto (+20), urgency and secrecy tactics (+12 each), suspicious look-alike links (+15–20), and requests for your SSN or bank details (+12) stack up fast — and dangerous combinations (agency name plus a payment demand) add bonus points, because that's the classic scam shape.

    Verdict thresholds: 70–100 = High risk — likely a scam · 35–69 = Caution · 0–34 = Low risk.

    This is a heuristic screen, not proof. It looks for known scam patterns — it cannot verify who really sent a message, and a clever new scam may score low. The rules that never change: legitimate agencies never demand gift cards, crypto, or wire transfers; never use contact details from the suspicious message itself; when in doubt, hang up and verify independently.

                  Scam text checker FAQ

                  Will the real FBI ever call me?

                  No. The FBI says law enforcement will never call, text, or email to demand payment, gift cards, cryptocurrency, or personal information. Anyone claiming to be the FBI who asks for money or your Social Security number is a scammer — hang up and report it at ic3.gov.

                  How can I check if a text message is a scam?

                  Paste it into the checker above. It scans for the red flags the FBI warns about — fake agency names, arrest threats, gift-card or crypto demands, manufactured urgency, and look-alike links — and returns a 0–100 risk score instantly, all inside your browser.

                  What should I do with a suspicious text?

                  Don't reply, click, or call back. Screenshot it, forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM), and report it at ic3.gov. If you already sent money, call your bank's fraud line immediately.

                  Is this scam text checker private?

                  Yes. It runs entirely in your browser — pasted text is never sent to a server, stored, or logged. It works even if you disconnect from the internet after the page loads.

                  What does an FBI impersonation scam look like?

                  A call, text, or email claiming you're under federal investigation or have an arrest warrant, demanding immediate payment by gift card, crypto, or wire — and warning you not to tell anyone. Scammers spoof real office numbers and use real agents' names.

                  Can a scam call really show the FBI's number?

                  Yes — scammers spoof caller ID, so your screen can show a real FBI office number during a fake call. Never trust the incoming number: hang up and call the agency on a number you look up yourself.
