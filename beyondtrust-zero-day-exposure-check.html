What the attacker did (so you know what to hunt)

Per Mandiant's analysis of the August 29 exploitation: about 11 minutes after entry, the attacker dropped the mangoapi.php SAWFLY web shell, created a SUID copy of the system shell to become root, dumped the credential vault, added a boot-time persistence script with a passwordless sudo rule, and wiped local logs. Then, using a compromised administrator account, it raised Jump Item and team roles to Administrator, opened support sessions to internal endpoints, pulled documents through built-in file transfer, and tunneled out via the BARECLAW tunneler.

Hunt these indicators

  • mangoapi.php — SAWFLY web shell (and app_84304470%2Ephp staging paths in web logs)
  • ingredi_ws in /tmp — BARECLAW tunneler listening on TCP 19800, path /uidchanarge
  • /usr/bin/dbus-authd — unexpected root-owned SUID shell copy
  • /home/bomgar/sbin/post_boot.d/zz-root-persist — boot persistence adding passwordless sudo
  • HAProxy config tampering — backend webskd3, ACL wswsws routing /uidchanarge to 127.0.0.1:19800
  • Syslog user_changed events raising Jump Item roles to Administrator without a change ticket

October 19 update: Mandiant names the attacker — Armory Castle

October 19, 2026 — Mandiant researchers now give the actor a name: the likely Chinese government-backed espionage group it previously tracked as UNC5221 is now called ARMORY CASTLE (also tracked by CrowdStrike as Warp Panda / UTA0178). Alongside the naming comes a fuller picture of the group's behavior inside the compromised appliances: after landing the zero-day, it showcased privilege escalation, lateral movement — "hop around the compromised network" — and detection-evasion tactics.

Armory Castle's track record is a pattern of sweeping attack sprees against edge and remote-access appliances:

  • Ivanti, repeatedly since 2023 — a series of Ivanti Connect Secure defects (CVE-2023-46805, CVE-2024-21887, CVE-2025-0282)
  • CitrixBleed — the Citrix NetScaler zero-day (CVE-2023-4966)
  • Brickstorm — last year's espionage campaign, with an average attacker dwell time of roughly 400 days — some 9,600 hours of undetected access per victim
  • BeyondTrust — CVE-2026-102764, exploited as a zero-day from August 29, 2026

Active since October 2023, the group targets technology, legal services, SaaS providers, business-process outsourcers and government bodies — sectors chosen for long-term intelligence collection. Its playbook is consistent: exploit a zero-day in an internet-facing appliance narrowly and quietly against high-value targets, then pivot to broad, opportunistic exploitation once the vulnerability goes public. Mandiant's warning stands: "prepare for potential widespread exploitation." GTIG senior analyst Kelli Vanderlee adds that "we are aware of only a small number of impacted organizations at this time, but given the stealth of the activity, and the use of a zero-day, it is possible that additional organizations were also compromised prior to the disclosure."

FAQ

What is BeyondTrust advisory BT26-05?

BT26-05 is the BeyondTrust security advisory published October 15, 2026 for CVE-2026-102764, a critical BeyondTrust zero-day vulnerability (CVSS 4.0 9.3, CWE-290 improper origin validation) in BeyondTrust Remote Support. It lets an unauthenticated attacker perform file operations on the appliance, which attackers turned into root access.

Was the BeyondTrust zero-day exploited in the wild?

Yes. BeyondTrust and Mandiant report CVE-2026-102764 was exploited as a zero-day starting August 29, 2026 against one BeyondTrust-hosted and one self-hosted Remote Support instance by the suspected China-nexus espionage actor ARMORY CASTLE, which deployed the SAWFLY web shell and BARECLAW tunnel, escalated to root, and wiped logs.

Which BeyondTrust versions are affected by CVE-2026-102764?

All BeyondTrust Remote Support versions 26.2.3 and earlier are affected. The BT26-05-RS patch covers versions 24.3.1 through 26.2.3; appliances older than 24.3.1 must upgrade first, then apply the patch. A version number alone does not confirm the patch is present.

What is the CISA KEV due date for CVE-2026-102764?

CISA added CVE-2026-102764 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog on October 16, 2026 with a binding remediation due date of October 19, 2026 and flagged forensic triage as required for potentially exposed appliances.

Does patching BT26-05 mean my appliance is clean?

No. The BT26-05-RS patch closes the entry point, but an appliance that was internet-reachable during the August 29 to October 8 window may already be compromised. CISA's KEV listing flags forensic triage as required: hunt the Mandiant indicators, preserve encrypted appliance logs, rotate the credential vault, and rebuild through BeyondTrust support rather than just deleting a web shell.

Who is Armory Castle (UNC5221)?

Armory Castle is Mandiant's name (October 2026) for a suspected China-nexus espionage group it previously tracked as UNC5221 — also tracked by CrowdStrike as Warp Panda / UTA0178 — active since October 2023. It specializes in zero-day exploitation of edge and remote-access appliances: Ivanti defects repeatedly since 2023, the CitrixBleed NetScaler zero-day, last year's Brickstorm campaign (average attacker dwell time ~400 days), and now the BeyondTrust CVE-2026-102764 zero-day. Once inside, it escalates privileges, moves laterally, and evades detection.
