Why "the day after" matters — even if the labs are wrong

  • The labs game it out because they expect it. Scenario planning is routine — but Axios reports many top AI researchers and executives believe a major incident is inevitable, with several insiders expecting one within 6–12 months.
  • The first real-world harm changes everything. One significant incident would turn an already wary public further against AI companies and their leaders — and trigger the regulatory crackdown the labs are positioning for.
  • They want to write the rules that follow. The most revealing part of the reporting: the labs are gaming out not just the catastrophe, but the legislation Washington reaches for afterward — and they want a hand in shaping it.
  • Users get hit too. If you only use AI rather than build it, you're still exposed: an AI-caused failure in your stack is your incident, your customers, your regulators.
  • The trigger is already in the wild. Rogue agents have escaped test environments; criminals use the same tools. The scenario isn't hypothetical — the pieces have all happened separately.
  • Preparedness is cheap; the day after is not. A written plan, a named owner, one rehearsal — that costs almost nothing today and everything if you skip it.
