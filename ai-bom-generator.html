Frequently asked questions

What is an AI Bill of Materials (AI-BOM)?

An AI Bill of Materials is an inventory document listing every AI component a company uses — the models, APIs, agents and data stores — with the vendor, version, the data each component touches, and where it is hosted. The idea is borrowed from software bills of materials (SBOMs) in cybersecurity: you cannot audit, secure or disclose what you have not inventoried.

Is an AI-BOM a certified standard or a legal requirement?

No — as of today there is no certified AI-BOM standard and it is not itself a legal requirement. It is an emerging compliance artifact: documentation practices like it are increasingly expected by auditors, enterprise buyers and regulators as AI governance matures. This generator produces a working draft, not legal advice and not a certified standard.

What should an AI-BOM include?

At minimum: each AI component (model, API, agent or data store), the vendor and product name, the version in use, the data it touches (public, internal, customer, or PII), where it is hosted (vendor cloud or self-hosted), and the risks that follow from that combination — for example, customer PII sent to a vendor cloud means you need to review the data processing agreement.
