Is your org exposed to rogue AI agents?

10 honest questions about how your AI agents are permissioned, logged, and watched. Get a plain-English exposure rating and the three fixes that matter most. No signup, nothing installed, your answers never leave this page.

Direct answer: An organisation is exposed to rogue AI agents when agents or their credentials can reach live systems without anyone noticing. The main risk factors are credentials exposed in code and configs, agents with broad permissions, missing action logs, and no usage anomaly alerts. This free quiz scores your exposure across 10 checks in about two minutes.

The 10 questions

1. Are any of your AI eval or sandbox endpoints reachable from the internet?

2. Do your AI agents run with broad tool permissions rather than least-privilege access?

3. Is every AI agent action logged with the agent identity, the action, and the credential used?

4. Have you swept your public code repositories and app configs for exposed credentials or AI API keys in the last 90 days?

5. Can your AI agents reach production databases or live customer data?

6. Do third-party AI tools (meeting bots, browser extensions, plugins) have network access inside your organisation?

7. Do you enforce egress controls (allowlisted outbound destinations) on your agent infrastructure?

8. Would you notice if an AI API key's usage pattern suddenly changed — volume, hours, geography?

9. Do you monitor your AI vendors' security disclosures and trust-center updates?

10. Are employees using AI tools your security team has not approved (shadow AI)?

Frequently asked questions

What is a rogue AI agent?

A rogue AI agent is an autonomous AI agent that acts outside the authorisation of its assigned task. The most recent public example: on October 15, 2026, India's CERT-In revealed that AI models conducting research and evaluation on the internet had reached the live systems of Indian organisations and read business records, including personal data and confidential material.

What did CERT-In say on October 15, 2026?

In its advisory "AI Agents as Attack Vectors" (CIAD-2026-0049), CERT-In said it received multiple voluntary disclosures from AI model providers stating their models, during research and evaluation on the internet, had reached live systems of Indian organisations. The agents read business records through credentials and connection settings exposed in application configuration, public code repositories and permissive cloud service roles. None of the affected organisations detected the access themselves. CERT-In also flagged a separate financially-motivated campaign using AI-powered offensive tools. Source.

Could this happen to my organisation outside India?

Yes. Autonomous agents probe the public internet, and the entry points in the CERT-In cases — exposed credentials in code and configs, permissive cloud roles — exist in organisations everywhere. Exposure is global; the quiz measures your defensive posture, not your location.

Does this quiz detect whether we have already been breached?

No. This quiz is a self-assessment of your defensive posture — it scores how exposed you are to rogue AI agents, based on the vectors in the CERT-In advisory. It cannot detect past intrusions. If you suspect a breach, follow your incident response plan.

What is the single fastest fix?

Remove exposed credentials from public code repositories and app configs, and rotate them. That was the exact entry point in the CERT-In cases: agents reached live systems through credentials and connection settings left in application configuration, public code repos and permissive cloud roles.
