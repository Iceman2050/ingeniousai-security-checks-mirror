Next.js CVE checker: two unauthenticated RCEs — are you patched?

Next.js just shipped an out-of-band security update for two critical unauthenticated remote-code-execution flaws (CVSS 9.5 each) plus a high-severity denial-of-service bug. Patch now — the order matters

  • Upgrade Next.js to 16.4.1 (Active LTS) or 15.5.28 (Maintenance LTS). Upgrading pulls a fixed sharp automatically.
  • Can't upgrade today? For the SVG RCE only: pin sharp ≥ 0.35.5 or block SVG loads in sharp. There is no workaround for the use cache RCE — that one needs the upgrade.
  • Audit your exposure: grep for use cache functions returning typed arrays, check for dangerouslyAllowSVG or next/og usage, inventory your Server Actions.

FAQ

What are the Next.js October 2026 critical vulnerabilities?

Next.js shipped an out-of-band update on October 14, 2026 fixing three flaws: (1) unauthenticated RCE via 'use cache' functions returning multiple typed arrays (GHSA-673q-mjm6-r5xr, CVSS 9.5) in 16.0.0–16.4.0 with Cache Components; (2) unauthenticated RCE via SVG image processing through sharp/librsvg (CVE-2026-96889, CVSS 9.5) in 10.0.0–15.5.27 and 16.0.0–16.4.0 on glibc Linux; (3) high-severity DoS via Server Actions (GHSA-p3m5-59mp-jv5r) in 13.0.0–15.5.27 and 16.0.0–16.4.0. Fixed in 16.4.1 and 15.5.28.

How do I check my Next.js version?

Run 'npm ls next' in your project, or check the version in package.json / package-lock.json. Then compare against the fixed releases: 16.4.1 (Active LTS) and 15.5.28 (Maintenance LTS). The interactive checker on this page does the comparison for you — paste your version and it tells you which of the three flaws you're exposed to.

Is there a workaround for the Next.js SVG RCE without upgrading?

Partially: ensure sharp is 0.35.5 or later, or block SVG rendering in sharp with sharp.block({ operation: ['VipsForeignLoadSvg'] }). But the 'use cache' RCE has no workaround — Vercel recommends upgrading even if you believe your app doesn't use the affected pattern. No public exploitation had been reported as of October 15, 2026.

What is a CVE checker?

A CVE checker is a free tool that tells you whether your software is affected by a published CVE — a publicly known cybersecurity vulnerability. It compares your version or configuration against the official advisory and gives you a patched, vulnerable, or unknown verdict in about 30 seconds.

How do I check if my software has a CVE?

Find your exact version number, then compare it against the vendor's security advisory or the NVD entry for the CVE. The fastest way is a CVE checker built for that product: paste your version and get an instant verdict against the published version tables.

How do I know if I am affected by a CVE?

You are affected when you run a vulnerable version and the vulnerable component is reachable or enabled — for example, open sign-ups, an exposed management port, or a vulnerable app installed. A CVE checker gives you the version verdict in seconds; the advisory's conditions tell you whether that verdict means real exposure.
