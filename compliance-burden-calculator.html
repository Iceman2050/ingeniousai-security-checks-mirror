FAQ

What are the EU AI Act fine tiers?

The EU AI Act has three fine tiers (Article 99): up to €35 million or 7% of worldwide turnover for prohibited AI practices; up to €15 million or 3% for most breaches including Article 50 transparency obligations; up to €7.5 million or 1% for misleading information. SMEs pay the lower of the flat amount or the percentage.

What are the fines for EU AI Act non-compliance?

Breaches of the Article 50 transparency obligations fall under the middle fine band: up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Violations of the prohibited practices in Article 5 reach up to €35 million or 7%. Small and medium enterprises benefit from a lower-of rule on these ceilings.

Which EU AI Act obligations apply to chatbots?

Chatbots trigger Article 50: users must be told they are interacting with AI at first interaction, unless it is obvious from context. If the chatbot generates synthetic content, that content must also be marked as AI-generated.

Does the EU AI Act apply to small companies?

Yes — the Act applies regardless of company size when AI systems are placed on the EU market. SMEs get some relief, including a lower-of rule on fine ceilings and simplified measures under the Digital Omnibus, but the transparency obligations still apply.

When did the EU AI Act transparency rules become enforceable?

The Article 50 transparency obligations became applicable on 2 August 2026, and national authorities can enforce them now. Machine-readable marking requirements for already-deployed systems have a transition period running to 2 December 2026.

Key facts — EU AI Act enforcement

  • Fine tiers (Article 99): up to €35M or 7% of worldwide turnover for prohibited practices (Art. 5); up to €15M or 3% for most obligations including Article 50 transparency breaches; up to €7.5M or 1% for misleading information to authorities. SMEs and start-ups pay the lower of the flat amount or the percentage.
  • Enforcement timeline: prohibition and fine provisions applicable since 2 August 2025; Article 50 transparency obligations applicable since 2 August 2026; machine-readable marking for already-deployed systems transitions through 2 December 2026.
