Key facts

  • 5 clauses decide your exposure: training rights, data retention, liability and indemnity, subprocessors, and the change-notice clause itself.
  • 'Continued use constitutes acceptance' is the standard mechanism — silence is consent, so an unread change is still a binding one.
  • An archived copy is evidence: without a dated snapshot of the old terms, you cannot prove what changed or when.

Which terms clauses actually matter for ai governance?

Five clauses carry almost all of the practical risk in an AI vendor's terms. Training rights decide whether your inputs improve the vendor's models; retention decides how long your data lives on their servers; liability decides who pays when the model is wrong; subprocessors decide where else your data flows; and the change clause decides how you will find out.

  • 1. Training rights — search 'train'. 'We may use your content to train or improve our models' is a flag; an explicit no-training promise on your tier is worth screenshotting with today's date.
  • 2. Data retention — search 'retain'. Retention measured in months with vague deletion language is a flag, especially for regulated data.
  • 3. Liability and indemnity — search 'indemnif'. If you indemnify the vendor for outputs you publish, a hallucination about your customer becomes your bill.
  • 4. Subprocessors — search 'subprocessor'. A long list you never reviewed means your data travels further than the vendor's logo suggests.
  • 5. Change notice — search 'modif' or 'changes to these terms'. 'We may update at any time; continued use constitutes acceptance' means the page itself is the notice.

How should a small team run a terms watch?

  1. List your vendors — every AI API, chatbot and embedded AI feature your product depends on, with the exact terms and privacy URLs.
  2. Snapshot the current text of each page with a date, so any later diff has a baseline.
  3. Review on a cadence — monthly for core vendors, quarterly for the rest; re-run the 5-clause check on every change.
  4. Act on flags: opt out of training where the vendor offers controls, move sensitive workloads to tiers that contractually exclude training, and record the decision.
  5. Escalate contract-affecting changes to whoever owns vendor risk — a terms change can quietly rewrite a promise you made your own customers.

This is a layperson's triage pass, not legal advice — it exists so the changes reach a human before they become a surprise.

Frequently asked questions

What is an AI vendor terms change?

It is any edit a vendor makes to its terms of service or privacy policy after you signed up — most importantly changes to training rights, data retention, liability or subprocessors. Vendors usually announce by updating the page itself, and standard change clauses make continued use of the product count as acceptance.

Does my AI vendor train on my data?

It depends on the vendor and your plan tier, and the answer lives in clause 1 of the current terms. Run the training-rights check on the live terms page: if the text allows training on your content, look for the vendor's data-controls or opt-out setting, or move to a tier that contractually excludes training.

How often do AI vendors change their terms?

Often enough that an annual read is not a control. Major vendors revise terms and privacy policies multiple times a year, and the edits that matter rarely come with a fanfare email. A monthly snapshot-and-diff rhythm on core vendors is the smallest cadence that reliably catches them.
