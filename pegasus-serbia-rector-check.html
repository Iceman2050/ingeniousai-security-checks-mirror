Cybersecurity News Today: Pegasus Confirmed on a University Rector's iPhone in Serbia

The spyware story in cybersecurity news today is not a warning about a possible infection. It is a confirmation. Citizen Lab and the SHARE Foundation announced on October 19 that the iPhone of Vladan Đokić, rector of the University of Belgrade, was infected with NSO Group's Pegasus through a zero-click exploit: no tap, no link, no file. Đokić backs the student protest movement that has filled Serbia's streets since late 2024. The announcement landed six days before Serbia's October 25 snap parliamentary election. A second case in the same report is handled differently by the researchers, and the difference between the two is the most useful thing on this page.

Honest scope — read this first: One infection is confirmed (the rector's, with high confidence). The second case, a prosecutorial associate, is targeting only: he received Apple Threat Notifications, and forensic analysis of his device is still running, so no one honest can call him infected yet. The researchers named no operator: who ran the infection is under investigation, and this page does not guess. Mercenary spyware is aimed at specific people in specific roles; it is not circulating among ordinary phone owners.

Two cases, two different levels of proof

  • Vladan Đokić — infection confirmed. Rector of the University of Belgrade and a public supporter of the student protests. Forensic work by Citizen Lab and SHARE confirmed Pegasus on his iPhone with high confidence, delivered by a zero-click exploit at some point in 2026; the exact date is not public. Đokić called the infection intimidation and demanded an independent investigation.
  • Rade Bajić — targeting indicated, infection unconfirmed. A senior prosecutorial associate at the Public Prosecutor's Office for Organized Crime, who worked on the "Generalštab" corruption investigation into a Belgrade property project linked to Jared Kushner. Apple sent him Threat Notifications on August 13 and a reminder on August 27. Citizen Lab counts that as a high-confidence sign of likely targeting; whether his device was actually infected is still being established.
  • The count: with these two, at least 16 people in Serbia are now publicly identified as targeted or infected. SHARE had already reported at least 14 targeted since early 2026.
  • The timing: the confirmation was announced October 19. Serbia votes in a snap parliamentary election on October 25, called after the protest wave that followed the November 2024 Novi Sad station canopy collapse, which killed 16 people.

A zero-click exploit gives the victim nothing to refuse: 100 percent of the work happens on the attacker's side, inside the code that processes a message before it is even opened. That is why advice built for phishing does not apply here, and why the response below is about device settings and evidence, not about spotting a suspicious link.

Why this cybersecurity news today matters: the notification is the early warning

Apple's Threat Notifications exist for exactly this pattern. They go to a small number of users Apple believes were individually targeted by state-sponsored attackers, and in the Serbian case the August notifications reached the prosecutor's associate weeks before any public reporting. Read the two cases together and the system becomes clear: the notification is the early warning, the forensic analysis is the confirmation, and the gap between them is where the right actions live. Wiping the phone in that gap destroys the evidence. Ignoring the notification wastes the warning. The check below sorts out which situation you are in.

The threat-notification action check

Three answers. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

If a notification reaches you, run these steps

  • Believe it: Apple sends Threat Notifications only when it believes a specific user was individually targeted by a state-sponsored attacker. It is not a marketing message or a software bug.
  • Turn on Lockdown Mode: Settings, Privacy and Security, Lockdown Mode. It restricts message attachments, link previews and other surfaces that zero-click exploits abuse.
  • Update iOS the same day: each release closes known exploit paths. A phone behind on updates carries doors that are already documented.
  • Do not wipe the phone: a factory reset feels decisive and destroys the forensic evidence that confirms the infection and identifies the method. Get expert advice first.
  • Get specialist help: Access Now's Digital Security Helpline handles targeted-surveillance cases, and groups in the Citizen Lab and SHARE orbit do the forensic work. This is their daily job, not a general IT ticket.
  • Move sensitive life to a clean device: change passwords and move sensitive accounts and conversations from a device you trust, not from the phone that may be compromised.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Pegasus Serbia case, in plain terms?

The Pegasus Serbia case is the October 2026 confirmation by Citizen Lab and the SHARE Foundation that NSO Group's Pegasus spyware infected the iPhone of Vladan Đokić, rector of the University of Belgrade, through a zero-click exploit. A second official, prosecutor's associate Rade Bajić, received Apple threat notifications in August; researchers treat that as likely targeting, with his infection not confirmed.

Does an Apple threat notification mean my phone is infected?

No. It means Apple believes you were targeted by a state-sponsored attacker, which is a different claim from a confirmed infection. Citizen Lab treats these notifications as a high-confidence sign of likely targeting. Infection itself is established by forensic analysis of the device, which is why the Serbian prosecutor's associate is described as targeted while his case is still being examined. A notification is still an instruction to act: turn on Lockdown Mode, update the phone, and get expert help.

How does Pegasus infect a phone with no click?

Through zero-click exploits: flaws in the code that processes incoming messages, images or calls, which run when the content arrives, before the owner opens anything. There is no link to avoid and no attachment to refuse, which is what separates mercenary spyware from ordinary phishing. The defenses are structural: install system updates promptly, since each one closes known exploit paths, and use Lockdown Mode, which shrinks the attack surface by restricting message attachments, link previews and other features these exploits abuse.

Who gets targeted with this kind of spyware?

People whose work draws the attention of a state or a state client: officials, prosecutors, journalists, lawyers, academics and activists in public disputes. The Serbian cases fit that shape: a university rector backing a protest movement and a prosecutorial associate working a corruption investigation tied to a politically sensitive property project. With these two, at least 16 people in Serbia are publicly identified as targeted or infected. This is not a mass-consumer threat; it is aimed, one person at a time, which is why the per-person response matters.

What should I do if I receive a threat notification?

Treat it as real. Turn on Lockdown Mode and update iOS the same day. Do not wipe or factory-reset the phone before getting forensic advice, because the device carries the evidence that confirms what happened. Contact a digital security helpline such as Access Now's, which handles exactly these cases. Move sensitive accounts and conversations to a clean device and change passwords from that device, not from the phone that may be compromised. Then follow the steps in the check on this page.
