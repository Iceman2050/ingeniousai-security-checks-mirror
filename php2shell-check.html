Cybersecurity News Today: PHP2Shell — Two Critical PHP RCE Flaws, Fixes Shipped, Attacks Already Live

The most urgent story in cybersecurity news today is a flaw in the language that runs much of the web. Two critical vulnerabilities in the PHP runtime itself — tracked as CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 and nicknamed PHP2Shell by researchers — allow unauthenticated remote code execution: an attacker who can reach your website can run code on your server without logging in. Both flaws are rated CVSS 9.2 by the PHP security team. PHP shipped an emergency out-of-cycle release on October 21, 2026 after exploit details leaked ahead of the planned disclosure — and Cloudflare reports it has already observed attack traffic. If your site runs on PHP (WordPress, Drupal, Joomla, Magento, Laravel, or anything custom), the check below takes under a minute.

Honest scope — read this first: The fixes exist and are downloadable now: PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36, and 8.2.35. If your server already runs one of those exact versions, you are done. The exposed population is everyone else: supported branches that have not updated yet, and the much larger group still running PHP 8.1 or older, which receives no upstream fix at all. Two more things worth knowing before you panic or relax: on Apache with mod_php — the setup PHP considers highest risk — a demonstrated exploit fires before your application code runs, so no plugin, framework middleware, or input validation in your app can filter it out; and as of October 21 the flaws are not yet in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog (per watchTowr's tracking), which is a statement about catalog timing, not about whether attacks are happening — Cloudflare says they are.

The two flaws, exactly as disclosed

  • CVE-2026-109618 — RCE via out-of-memory corruption (GHSA-w8hw-w9fx-j79w): when an out-of-memory condition interrupts PHP before request data is fully initialized, the partially initialized state can be turned into code execution.
  • CVE-2026-109619 — RCE via stale global state (GHSA-6wmf-4rv5-p3rp): internal state from one request can survive into a later request handled by the same worker process, and that leftover state can be turned into code execution.
  • Who disclosed them: the flaws are credited to Calif.io, Wiz, OpenAI, and Quarkslab. The name "PHP2Shell" comes from researchers, not from the PHP project.
  • Scope, per PHP's advisory: all PHP installations serving web requests — PHP-FPM, Apache with mod_php, php-cgi, FrankenPHP, and LiteSpeed. Risk is highest for mod_php, where the demonstrated exploit is triggered by an unauthenticated request before any application code executes.

Put the exposure in proportion: about 884,000 internet-facing hosts — 59 percent of the roughly 1.5 million hosts that reported any PHP version to Shodan on October 20 — reported running PHP 8.1 or older, the versions that get no upstream fix. Banner data is not a confirmed-affected list, and a further ~740,000 hosts reported PHP without a version at all — but even with those caveats, end-of-life PHP is plainly the largest part of the problem.

Why this cybersecurity news today matters: a leak, a race, and a pre-code exploit

The timeline is why this became an emergency. On October 20, a post claimed two PHP RCE flaws would be disclosed on October 22 — with a CVSS 10.0 figure and a claim that everything since PHP 5 was affected. Researchers at watchTowr confirmed the flaws were real and under embargo; the 10.0 score was the leak's number, PHP's own rating is 9.2, and the "everything since PHP 5" scope was not confirmed by any vendor advisory at leak time. PHP moved the release up a day, publishing its advance notice at 19:33 UTC on October 20 and shipping the fixes on October 21. Meanwhile the defensive race had already started: Cloudflare said it observed attackers probing its customers and mutating payloads to evade rules, and switched on WAF protections for free and paid customers at 03:02 UTC — hours before the patches landed. For comparison, when the PHP CGI flaw CVE-2024-4577 became public in June 2024, Akamai saw exploit attempts against its honeypots within 24 hours. PHP flaws of this class do not get a grace period.

The defensive response then escalated to something rarely seen: governments taking their own websites offline on purpose. CERT-EU, the European Union's cyber emergency team, advised organisations that could not apply the fixes to consider taking affected websites offline — and many did, preventively, ahead of the 14:00 UTC release. In the Netherlands, following the example of the country's own national cyber security centre, organisations pulled public sites offline including the Port of Rotterdam, the government portal Overheid.nl, the national football association KNVB, the websites of the country's five largest political parties, and several hospital websites. In Finland, the state IT provider Valtori took government public-network services offline as a precaution. In France, the Interior Ministry temporarily took local-government prefecture websites offline while awaiting a fix. In Germany, the federal IT provider ITZBund took several websites offline — a government spokesman stressed the move was purely preventive and that no security incidents were known. In Denmark, the municipal IT company Kombit shut down several services, including the national school communication platform. The US National Security Agency gave similar advice, urging organisations that cannot update immediately to take affected systems offline until the flaws are mitigated. To be clear about what this wave was: precautionary shutdowns by defenders racing a disclosure clock — not confirmed breaches.

Two operational details that decide whether the update actually protects you. First, restart the PHP workers after updating: a web-server reload alone can leave detached PHP-FPM or LiteSpeed workers running the old, vulnerable build — verify the new version answers real web requests, not just the CLI. Second, Linux distributions and LTS vendors received patches in advance, so a distribution package with a backported fix counts — but verify it explicitly; PHP noted that the volunteer-maintained Remi repository packages would not have updates on release day, and pointed users to distribution packages or source builds instead.

Check your version — the 30-second PHP2Shell verdict

Pick your PHP branch and patch number (the x.y.z part of your version — from php -v or your hosting panel). Everything runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

Distro builds: read the changelog, not the version string. Linux distributions and LTS vendors often ship the fix inside a package that keeps an older-looking version number. If your PHP comes from a distribution package, the package changelog — checked for CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 — is the authority on whether you are patched, not the bare version this checker compares. That is Debian's published advice for exactly this situation, and it overrides a "vulnerable" verdict below.

Run the PHP2Shell check now

  • Find your real version: run php -v on the server, or read it in your hosting panel. Then check it against the table above — or use the checker. If a host or developer manages your server, get the exact version in writing.
  • Know your SAPI: if PHP runs as Apache mod_php, you are in PHP's highest-risk group — the exploit fires before your application code, so patch first and analyze later. PHP-FPM, php-cgi, FrankenPHP, and LiteSpeed are in scope too; none of them is a safe harbor.
  • Update, then restart the workers: install 8.5.12 / 8.4.27 / 8.3.36 / 8.2.35 (or your distribution's verified backport), restart PHP-FPM / LiteSpeed / Apache workers — a bare reload can leave old workers alive — and confirm the new version through a real web request. Then re-check your memory settings: the fix introduces a new hard memory limit, and when a request hits it the process ends on the spot and shutdown handlers do not run — so confirm memory_limit and post_max_size still match what your application actually needs, or the patch can break legitimate work.
  • On PHP 8.1 or older: there is no upstream patch coming. Upgrade branches, take a verified distro/LTS backport, or apply the source backports PHP published for 7.4, 8.0, and 8.1 if you build from source. The UK's NCSC advises organisations on PHP 5 onward to act immediately — and to consider taking a system offline if it cannot be updated or shielded quickly.
  • If you cannot patch today: put the application behind a WAF (Cloudflare's PHP2Shell rules have been live since 03:02 UTC on October 21, including on its free tier) or isolate internet-facing PHP from public networks, as CERT-EU advises. The advisory's configuration measures — OOM exposure-reduction guidance and request-size constraints, the session upload-progress setting, and on PHP 8.5 setting fatal_error_backtraces=0 — reduce some attack paths only. PHP is explicit that they are not a complete mitigation. One more warning from the advisory: do not substitute RLIMIT_AS or RLIMIT_DATA for the memory setting — PHP warns those process limits can trigger the same vulnerable path you are trying to close.
  • Hunt for compromise before you patch over the evidence: watch for php-fpm, php-cgi, httpd, or apache2 processes launching shells, interpreters, or download tools; check the web root for new files and web shells, and look for new cron jobs, scheduled tasks, and accounts. The researchers also flag a quieter pattern worth investigating — signs to dig into, not proof by themselves: unusual multipart requests followed by memory-limit errors or repeated HTTP 500 responses, oddly large PHPSESSID response cookies, and PHP workers restarting with no explanation. If you find any of it: isolate the server and preserve the web server and PHP logs first.

Frequently asked questions

What is PHP2Shell, in plain terms?

PHP2Shell is the name security researchers gave to two critical vulnerabilities in the PHP runtime itself, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, both rated CVSS 9.2 by the PHP security team. CVE-2026-109618 is a remote code execution flaw triggered when an out-of-memory condition interrupts PHP before request data is fully initialized; CVE-2026-109619 lets internal state from one request survive into a later request handled by the same worker. Either can let an unauthenticated attacker run code on a web server. The name is the researchers', not PHP's; the flaws were disclosed by Calif.io, Wiz, OpenAI, and Quarkslab after exploit details leaked ahead of the coordinated release.

Which PHP versions are fixed, and which are affected?

PHP says every installation that serves web requests is at risk, across PHP-FPM, Apache mod_php, php-cgi, FrankenPHP, and LiteSpeed, with mod_php considered highest risk because a demonstrated exploit triggers before any application code runs. The fixes shipped October 21, 2026 are PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36, and 8.2.35 — any earlier release on those branches is affected. PHP 8.1 and older receive no upstream fix; the PHP security team provided minimal source-level backports for 7.4, 8.0, and 8.1 for people who build PHP from source, and some Linux distributions and LTS vendors ship their own backports.

How do I check what PHP version my site runs?

On the server itself, run php -v in a terminal. On shared hosting, the version appears in your hosting control panel (cPanel, Plesk, or your host's dashboard), usually under PHP settings. A temporary phpinfo page also shows it — view it once and delete it immediately; a public phpinfo page is itself an information leak. Managed hosting? Get the exact version in writing.

Are the PHP2Shell flaws being exploited already?

Attack traffic has been observed. Cloudflare said it saw attackers targeting its customers and mutating payloads to get around its rules, and its WAF protections went live at 03:02 UTC on October 21, 2026 for free and paid customers, hours ahead of the fix release. Huntress reported no exploitation in its partner environments as of October 20 and no known public proof of concept at that time, and watchTowr listed the flaws as not in CISA's KEV catalog as of October 21. Because exploit details leaked before the coordinated release, defenders are treating this as patch-today, not patch-this-month.

What if my server runs PHP 8.1 or older?

It will not receive an upstream PHP fix. In order of preference: upgrade to a supported branch (8.2 or newer) and then to its fixed release; take a verified backport from your Linux distribution or LTS vendor; if you build PHP from source, apply the PHP security team's minimal backports for 7.4, 8.0, or 8.1; or take the system off the public internet, or behind a WAF, until you can do one of the above. About 884,000 internet-facing hosts reported PHP 8.1 or older in Shodan banner data on October 20, 2026 — 59 percent of the hosts reporting any PHP version — so end-of-life PHP is the largest part of the exposed population.
