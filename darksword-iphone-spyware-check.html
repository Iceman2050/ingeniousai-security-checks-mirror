DarkSword iPhone Spyware: a Cybersecurity Check

Spyware that steals your iCloud Keychain passwords and crypto wallets — with no click needed, just from visiting a compromised website. Here's what DarkSword P7 does, who is at risk, and the one fix that protects you.

Triggered by a viral post by Andy Diaz (Oct 17, 2026). Findings below are from iVerify's Threat Intelligence Team, reported October 8, 2026 via 9to5Mac. Technical claims are attributed to the researchers.

What DarkSword P7 is

DarkSword P7 is a new variant of an iPhone compromise framework. iVerify's Threat Intelligence Team found it in August 2026 while investigating an infection on the iPhone of a financial-institution employee. The name comes from the p7_ variable prefix the threat actor used when modifying the original kit.

The DarkSword family was first identified in March 2026 by Google, Lookout, and iVerify as a framework chaining six iPhone vulnerabilities. Google said it was used by multiple commercial surveillance vendors and suspected state-sponsored actors, with campaigns observed in Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Malaysia, and Ukraine.

How the attack works — step by step

  1. You visit a compromised website. DarkSword P7 is delivered through malicious ads in watering-hole attacks. Victims were not necessarily targeted individually — drive-by exposure through compromised web content was enough.
  2. No click needed. The exploit chain escapes the browser sandbox, escalates to kernel privileges, and injects the implant into SpringBoard (the core iOS interface process that handles everything on screen).
  3. The implant phones home every 15 seconds. SpringBoard handles the command-and-control traffic — heartbeats plus 15+ granular commands for two-way control of the infected device.
  4. It harvests your most sensitive data (see below) — and in P7's new twist, it processes your stolen iCloud Keychain data on your own device in JSON format, "sorting passwords at the scene" instead of exfiltrating the raw keychain database for later cracking.

What it steals

  • iCloud Keychain — passwords, processed on-device to extract the usable ones fast.
  • Crypto wallets — imToken plus 25+ other wallet apps.
  • Notes — exfiltrated in full.
  • Photos — exfiltrated in full.
  • Two-way control — 15+ commands from the operator, with 15-second check-ins.

Censys researchers also found open directories on the attackers' command servers: 11 victims' seed phrases, 179 stolen-data directories, and a Chinese-language "exploit-as-a-service" admin panel listing agents and resellers.

Who is at risk — and the fix

At risk: iPhones running iOS 18.4 through 18.7 that are unpatched. P7 expanded coverage to iOS 18.7.

The exploit chain uses two vulnerabilities that Apple has already fixed:

  • CVE-2025-24201 — WebKit out-of-bounds write, CVSS 10.0, fixed in iOS 18.3.2.
  • CVE-2025-31200 — Core Audio memory corruption, CVSS 9.8, fixed in iOS 18.4.1.

Apple also released iOS 18.7.7 in April 2026 to protect devices staying on the 18.x branch.

The key message

Patched devices are protected. Updating your iPhone is the fix. If your iPhone is fully up to date, this exploit chain cannot touch it — the doors it walks through are already locked. This page's advice is simple and final: check for a software update right now.

Warning signs

  • Unexplained battery drain or data usage (15-second check-ins have a cost).
  • You visited sketchy streaming or download sites on your phone and things felt "off" afterward.
  • You installed a crypto wallet from anywhere other than the official App Store.

Honest caveat from the researchers: iVerify notes that previous DarkSword indicators of compromise are no longer valid — P7 is stealthier. The absence of symptoms is not proof of safety; the presence of the latest iOS is the protection.

Context: the bigger cybersecurity picture

  • Not vibe-coded. iVerify told 9to5Mac that unlike earlier DarkSword variants that looked AI-generated, P7 appears hand-crafted — real human effort went into this one.
  • A business, not a hobby. The Chinese-language exploit-as-a-service admin panel with agents and resellers shows this is commercialized capability, sold down a supply chain.
  • High-end targeting roots. The original DarkSword was linked to commercial surveillance vendors and suspected state actors — this variant extends that tradecraft to broader watering-hole delivery.

Frequently asked questions

What is DarkSword P7 iPhone spyware?

What does DarkSword P7 steal from an iPhone?
