AI disclosure — quick answers — FAQ

Does AI-generated content have to be labelled under the EU AI Act?

In most cases, yes. Deployers of AI-generated image, audio or video must disclose it as AI-generated — the Article 50(4) deployer disclosure duty has applied since 2 August 2026 — and providers must ensure AI outputs are marked so they are machine-readable and detectable.

What is the December 2, 2026 EU AI Act deadline?

From 2 December 2026, the AI Act's transparency and marking duties phase in for AI systems already on the market: providers must have AI-generated content marked so it is machine-readable and detectable. This is the date your disclosure evidence should already be in order.

What counts as AI-generated content under Article 50?

Article 50 covers AI systems that generate or manipulate image, audio or video content — including deepfakes — plus AI text published to inform the public on matters of public interest, and AI systems interacting directly with people (chatbots). Each category carries its own disclosure duty.

Do I need machine-readable marking, not just a visible label?

Yes, in addition to a clear visible notice at first exposure. Providers must mark AI outputs so they are machine-readable and detectable — for example watermarks, metadata, or C2PA Content Credentials. A caption alone does not cover the machine-readability duty.

Is an audit-trail log legal proof of compliance?

No. A dated evidence log shows diligence and helps you prepare for review, but it is not legal proof on its own. This page is a guidance tool — consult qualified counsel for the 2 December 2026 deadline and your specific obligations.
