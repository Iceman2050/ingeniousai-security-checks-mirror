SPYWARE & PHISHING — 5-QUESTION CHECK

The scam doesn't want one payment. It wants to live inside your device.

AI-enhanced spyware doesn't grab and run — it moves in: logging keystrokes, watching screens, learning which of your accounts are worth draining, and bleeding you slowly. Then comes the twist: a fake "please sign this document" email that harvests your signature, so criminals can forge it with the account numbers the spyware already stole. Five questions to check both doors.

Short answer: spyware gets in through malicious links and fake apps; the signature phish arrives as a DocuSign-style email. Defend both the same way — verify before you click, verify before you sign: open the signing app yourself instead of tapping the email link, and treat battery drain plus mystery apps as a compromise until proven otherwise.

What's verified, what's the reel's framing

  • URL-trick phishing is real and measured: Check Point documented ~200,000 phishing emails abusing URL userinfo tricks (hiding the real domain after an @) — the links look legitimate to users and slip past some security filters.
  • E-signature phishing is a documented wave: Check Point caught 40,000+ phishing emails impersonating SharePoint and e-signature services, funneled through abused redirect infrastructure to look authenticated.
  • The "slow bleed" spyware model — keyloggers that learn and exfiltrate over time — matches how modern infostealers operate; the reel's dramatization ("bleed you out financially") is color, the mechanism is real.
  • QR-code phishing ("quishing") in public places is covered in depth in our phishing quiz — parking-meter QR scams and all.

Four defenses that actually work

1. Never sign from the email

A real DocuSign request waits for you inside the DocuSign app or site. Open it yourself — type the address, don't tap the link. Unexpected document? Confirm with the sender on a separate channel first.

2. Read the URL before the page loads

Anything before an @ in a link is decoration — the real destination comes after it. Long-press links on mobile to preview where they actually go.

3. Audit for spyware signs monthly

Battery draining fast, data spikes, phone hot at idle, apps you don't recognize, accessibility services you didn't enable. One sign: investigate. Several: back up and factory-reset.

4. Keep the front door locked

OS and apps updated, no sideloaded apps, app-store-only installs, and a permission review every few months (see our phone permission audit). Spyware needs an install vector — don't provide one.
