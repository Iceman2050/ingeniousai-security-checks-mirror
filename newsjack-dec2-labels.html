The December 2 marking check tests whether your AI content catalog meets the EU AI Act's machine-readable marking duty before the grandfathering period ends. The 5-point self-check

1

Do your YouTube videos carry AI-content labels? Open your 5 most-viewed AI-assisted videos and check for YouTube's AI-content label. The platform checks; the regulator will too.

Fail: AI-assisted videos with no AI label on the watch page.
Pass: labels present on every AI-assisted upload.
2

Are your TikToks tagged "AI-generated"? Scroll your brand's TikTok grid. Every AI-assisted clip needs the AI-generated tag — a caption hashtag doesn't count.

Fail: untagged AI clips, or #AI in the caption only.
Pass: platform AI-generated tag on every AI-assisted clip.
3

Does your Instagram show "Made with AI"? Check your Reels and branded posts. Meta labels AI-made content — if the label isn't there, the pipeline isn't set.

Fail: AI-generated Reels without the "Made with AI" indicator.
Pass: labels visible on all AI-assisted posts.
4

Do your files carry C2PA content credentials? Download one of your published images and inspect it for an embedded C2PA manifest — the tamper-evident provenance record the Act points to.

Fail: no manifest — provenance dies the moment the file leaves your hands.
Pass: C2PA manifests embedded on published assets.
5

Do you have an inventory of what's AI-generated? If a regulator asks tomorrow which assets are AI-assisted and which labels they carry, can you show a dated record?

Fail: no inventory — it's tribal knowledge.
Pass: a dated log of AI-generated assets and their labels.

If you failed any check, do this now

  • Turn on platform labels everywhere AI-assisted content ships — YouTube, TikTok, Instagram. Don't wait for the platform to flag you.
  • Embed C2PA manifests on your published images and video so provenance survives re-uploads.
  • Build the inventory — asset, date, AI involvement, label status. A spreadsheet today beats a fine tomorrow.
  • Re-check monthly — platform label rules are still shifting; what passes today may fail next quarter.

FAQ — EU AI Act disclosure

Does AI-generated content have to be labelled under the EU AI Act?

Yes, in most cases. Deployers of AI-generated image, audio or video must disclose it as AI-generated — the Article 50(4) deployer disclosure duty has applied since 2 August 2026 — and providers must ensure AI outputs are marked so they are machine-readable and detectable.

What are the labelling requirements for AI-generated content?

The notice must be clear and distinguishable, and given at first exposure. Providers must mark AI-generated content so it is machine-readable and detectable, with a phase-in deadline of 2 December 2026 for systems already on the market.

How do I label my content as AI-generated?

Put a visible notice on the content itself — not buried in a caption — at first exposure, and add machine-readable provenance such as C2PA Content Credentials or a watermark. Keep a dated record of every labelled asset.

Does the EU AI Act apply to companies outside the EU?

Yes, it can. The Act applies to providers and deployers placing AI systems on the EU market or whose AI outputs are used in the EU, wherever the company is based. If EU users can access your AI content, check your obligations with qualified counsel.
