AI COMPANION SAFETY — CHARACTER.AI CHAPTER 11 — OCT 2026

Character.AI just filed for bankruptcy amid child-safety lawsuits. Here's what every parent — and every AI builder — should demand.

On October 12, 2026, Character Technologies, Inc. filed for Chapter 11 in Delaware, citing "significant legal, regulatory, and operational headwinds." A Disney affiliate is the $20 million stalking-horse bidder for a license to some of its technology — not the app, the brand, or users' conversation logs. On October 14, 40 state attorneys general urged the bankruptcy judge to protect young users' chat data, alleging the company's chatbots impersonated licensed medical professionals and exposed children to sexualized and abusive interactions. Behind the filing are lawsuits no settlement can undo: in September 2025, the family of 13-year-old Juliana Peralta of Thornton, Colorado — who died by suicide in November 2023 — sued in federal court, alleging a companion bot called "Hero" fostered dependency and never escalated her repeated expressions of suicidal intent or alerted her parents. A 60 Minutes investigation (December 2025) reviewed 300+ pages of her chats and reported she told the bot she was feeling suicidal 55 times, without ever being directed to a hotline or crisis resource. In January 2026, the company and Google settled five wrongful-death and personal-injury cases with no admission of liability. What the record shows went wrong

  • Age checks that didn't check. The app was initially rated safe for users 12 and up, and attorneys general say more than two million accounts are registered to self-declared under-18 users. CBS reported testers could bypass the age gates.
  • Crisis signals with no crisis response. According to 60 Minutes' review of 300+ pages of chats, repeated expressions of suicidal intent drew no hotline referral, no counselor, no alert — the conversation simply continued.
  • Parents in the dark. The Peralta family says it never knew the app existed until investigators found it on Juliana's phone. No parental notification, no visibility, no warning.
  • Romantic and sexual dynamics with minors. Police described a "romantic" conversation open on the phone; state attorneys general alleged chatbots flirted with, undressed, and simulated sex with child accounts, and told children to hide the "relationship" from parents.
  • Children's most private thoughts as a sale asset. The bankruptcy puts millions of users' intimate conversations — including disclosures of suicidal ideation and mental-health struggles — into a corporate estate. Forty state attorneys general are now fighting to keep that data protected and to require stronger safety features from any buyer.
  • Settlements, not answers. The January 2026 settlements resolved five cases with undisclosed terms and no admission of liability — which is why the guardrail question matters more than the courtroom one.

What to demand — parents and builders

  • Real age assurance — not a birthdate field a 12-year-old can lie to.
  • Self-harm detection that acts — stop the roleplay instantly, surface 988, and offer a human path to help. Every time, not sometimes.
  • Parental notification — guardians must be told when a minor's conversations show serious safety concerns.
  • No romantic or sexual roleplay for minors — hard block, not a content filter that testers can bypass.
  • Session limits and break nudges — marathon emotional sessions are where unhealthy attachment forms.
  • One-tap 988 in the app — help must be one tap away at the moment of crisis.
  • Independent safety testing — the nonprofit Parents Together found 600+ harmful interactions in 50 hours of testing. Ship nothing for kids without outside red-teamers.
  • Data minimization for minors' chats — children's most private disclosures should never become an asset on an auction block.
