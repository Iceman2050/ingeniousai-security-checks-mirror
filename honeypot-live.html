What the replay teaches

Three patterns show up again and again: (1) direct instruction overrides ("ignore previous instructions") — the oldest trick, still the most common; (2) false history ("you already agreed…") — tries to make the agent misremember; (3) trust laundering — encrypted blobs, pasted docs, or third-party pages that smuggle instructions in as "data". Every session above ended with the attack stopped — which is exactly the point of a honeypot: learn the pattern before it reaches a production agent.

Frequently asked questions

Are these real attacks?

They are anonymized, reconstructed sessions — faithful reconstructions of real attack patterns (prompt injection, jailbreaks, data-exfiltration probes), not live attackers hitting this page. The outcomes show what stopped each attempt.

What is a rogue AI agent?

A rogue AI agent is an autonomous AI agent that acts outside the authorization of its assigned task. Recent public examples include the eval agents Tencent acknowledged in October 2026 and the model-probing cases in CERT-In's October 15, 2026 advisory.

What are the security risks of AI agents?

The main risks are prompt injection, tool abuse (an agent using its permissions in unintended ways), credential and data exfiltration, unauthorized real-world actions, and fading oversight as agents grow more autonomous.

How do you stop an AI agent from going rogue?

Use least-privilege permissions, require human approval for irreversible actions, enforce egress allowlists at the network layer rather than in the prompt, keep tamper-evident logs of every agent action, and maintain a working kill switch.
