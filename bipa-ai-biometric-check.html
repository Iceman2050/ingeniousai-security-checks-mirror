Why is BIPA cybersecurity news for AI companies?

BIPA is cybersecurity news for AI companies because Illinois law sets fines up to $5,000 per violation for collecting biometric data without written consent — and AI products that scan faces or voices are prime targets. Several AI firms have already paid multi-million-dollar settlements. This 60-second check scores your exposure before regulators do.

  • Biometrics are the product, not a side effect. Face filters, avatars, voice cloning, and transcription all derive biometric identifiers by design — you can't "not notice" you're collecting them.
  • A selfie counts. The Grok suits allege a single uploaded selfie triggered collection. BIPA covers face geometry derived from a photo, not just a fingerprint scanner.
  • No injury needed. Since Rosenbach (2019), plaintiffs need only show the technical violation — no hacked database, no leaked data, no harm.
  • 900+ class actions filed. BIPA is the only US biometric law with a private right of action, which built a plaintiffs'-bar industry around it.
  • It follows the user. Out-of-state and foreign companies get sued. "We're not in Illinois" is not a defense if Illinois residents use your product.
  • The wave is accelerating. Paul Hastings warned in April 2026 of "a new wave of biometrics litigation" aimed squarely at AI companies.

What to do right now

  • Add a written biometric consent step before first collection — naming what you collect, why, and for how long. Not a ToS checkbox: a standalone, logged release.
  • Publish a retention & destruction schedule and actually delete on schedule — §15(a) requires both the policy and the practice.
  • Map every third party that touches biometric data (cloud vision APIs, analytics, subprocessors) and get consent covering disclosure — or stop the flow.
  • Minimize: if you only need a result (e.g. "face detected"), don't store the template. Less stored = less exposure.
  • Decide Illinois deliberately: either geo-exclude and document it, or comply fully. "We didn't think about it" is the fact pattern in every complaint.

FAQ

What does Illinois BIPA require of AI companies?

BIPA Section 15(b) requires private entities to give written notice and obtain a written release before collecting biometric identifiers — including face geometry, fingerprints, retina/iris scans, and voiceprints. Section 15(a) requires a publicly available retention and destruction schedule. No actual injury is needed to sue: the Illinois Supreme Court held in Rosenbach v. Six Flags (2019) that a bare statutory violation confers standing.

What are the penalties for violating BIPA?

$1,000 per negligent violation and $5,000 per intentional or reckless violation, plus attorneys' fees and injunctive relief. A 2024 amendment (SB 2979) limits accrual to the first unlawful scan or transmission per person, but with large user bases the exposure is still enormous — Facebook paid $650 million in 2020 and BNSF paid $75 million in 2024 to resolve BIPA class actions.

Why are AI companies being sued under BIPA?

AI products that analyze faces or voices — photo editing, avatars, face filters, voice cloning, meeting transcription — collect biometric identifiers as a core function. In October 2026, SpaceXAI (formerly xAI) was hit with two proposed class actions alleging its Grok platform illegally collected biometric information from an uploaded selfie without written notice. Google and Otter.ai face similar BIPA class-action claims. Defense firm Paul Hastings warned of 'a new wave of biometrics litigation' targeting AI companies.

Does BIPA apply if my company is not in Illinois?

Yes, if you collect biometric data from Illinois residents. BIPA follows the person, not the company — out-of-state and foreign AI companies have been sued under it. If your product has any Illinois users and touches face or voice biometrics, you are in scope.
