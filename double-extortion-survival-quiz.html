RANSOMWARE READINESS — OCT 2026

Could you survive a double-extortion attack?

Ransomware gangs don't just encrypt your files anymore. They steal your data first, then threaten to publish it on a dark-web leak site — so even perfect backups can't save you. Eight questions, 30 seconds: would your business survive?

Short answer: the double-extortion model — encrypt your systems AND threaten to leak your stolen data — means "we have backups" is no longer a complete answer. October 2026's victim list is fresh. Take the quiz, then fix the gaps before you're on next month's list.

How double extortion works

Move 1 — Encrypt. The gang breaks in (usually through stolen credentials or phishing), spreads across your network, and encrypts everything. No work gets done.
Move 2 — Exfiltrate. Before encrypting, they quietly steal your most sensitive data: client records, contracts, financials, employee files.
Move 3 — Squeeze. Pay for the decryption key, and pay again so they "don't" publish your data on their leak site. Restore from backup all you want — the stolen data is still in their hands.

Backups answer Move 1. Nothing in "we have backups" answers Moves 2 and 3. That's the gap this quiz measures.

Your 30-second win (do this today)

  • Turn on MFA on every remote entry point. VPN, RDP, email, admin logins — today. Stolen credentials are the most common way in, and MFA kills most of those attacks outright.
  • Verify one backup restore this week. Not "check the backup ran" — actually restore a file from an offline or immutable backup. Untested backups are a rumor.
  • Know when you're listed. If your data hit a leak site, would you find out this week — or from a journalist? Leak-site monitoring closes that gap.
