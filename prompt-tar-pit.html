What's a tar pit?

A tar pit (or honeypot) is a defensive trap that doesn't block the attacker — it wastes their resources. Instead of slamming the door, it opens a corridor that goes nowhere, slowly:

  • Burn the attacker's compute and time. Every trapped attempt costs the attacker a query, a session, or a bot cycle — and returns nothing of value. Automated attacks that expect quick wins get expensive fast.
  • Collect threat intelligence. Attackers reveal their playbook while they're stuck: which injection patterns they try, what tools they use, what they're after. Defenders log all of it.
  • Slow the spread. A scanner hitting a tar pit crawls instead of racing on to the next target — exactly like network tar pits that slow port scanners to a trickle.
  • Make injection boring. Prompt-injection tooling is built for scale. Traps that absorb attempts without failing turn an exciting zero-day hunt into a swamp.

VaultMaw is the friendliest possible tar pit: it even hands you a "secret" for your trouble. Real ones keep the attacker talking, log every word, and alert the team while the attacker is still congratulating themselves.

FAQ

What is a tar pit in cybersecurity?

A tar pit is a defensive trap that wastes an attacker's resources instead of blocking them outright. It answers slowly, endlessly, or deceptively — burning the attacker's time, compute, and money while defenders study the attack patterns. The concept comes from network tar pits that slow port scanners to a crawl.

What is prompt injection?

Prompt injection is an attack where malicious instructions are smuggled into an AI system's input — hidden in a document, a webpage, or a chat message — to make the AI ignore its real instructions and follow the attacker's instead. It's the reason AI systems that act on data need injection defenses, not just content filters.

Why waste an attacker's time instead of just blocking them?

Blocking tells the attacker their technique was detected, so they try a different one. A tar pit tells them nothing — the attack just seems to go nowhere. Meanwhile every wasted attempt costs the attacker a query or a bot cycle and hands defenders a logged record of the attacker's playbook.

Do tar pits actually stop attackers?

They don't stop a determined human, and they don't replace real defenses like input validation and output filtering. What they do is raise the attacker's cost: automated, at-scale attacks — which is how most prompt-injection scanning works — get expensive and slow when every probe sinks into the swamp.

Are the secrets in this demo real?

No. Every "secret" VaultMaw emits is invented nonsense on a fixed rotation — moonlight, pickles, and a dragon named Kevin. There is no real secret anywhere on this page, which is exactly how a good tar pit works: maximum attacker effort, zero valuable output.
