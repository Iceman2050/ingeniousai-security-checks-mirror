Frequently asked questions

Where do this calculator's numbers come from?

IBM's Cost of a Data Breach Report 2026, produced with the Ponemon Institute from 602 breached organizations across 16 countries, covering incidents between March 2025 and February 2026. Key published figures: $4.99M global average, $11.5M US average, $6.64M healthcare, $6.3M financial services, $5.2M energy, and $192 per customer PII record.

How accurate is this breach cost estimate?

It is a rough planning figure built from public averages, not a quote and not a prediction. IBM's report does not break results out by company size, and real breach costs vary widely with the data involved, the attack vector, and how fast the breach is contained.

Why does industry change the estimate?

Breach costs differ sharply by sector in IBM's data. Healthcare was the costliest industry for the 13th year running at $6.64M on average, followed by financial services at $6.3M and energy at $5.2M, against a $4.99M global average.

What actually reduces the cost of a breach?

Speed and tooling. Breaches contained within 200 days averaged $4.32M versus $5.65M for longer lifecycles, and organizations using security AI and automation extensively saved an average of $1.93M per breach (IBM, 2026).

What is the cost per record of a data breach?

IBM's 2026 report puts the average compromised customer PII record at $192, up from $160. Customer PII was the most commonly stolen data type at 52% of breaches; intellectual property was the costliest to lose at $196 per record.
