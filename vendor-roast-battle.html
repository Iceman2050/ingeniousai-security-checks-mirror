🔥 Contender 1 — OpenAI shrinks the $200 Pro plan

Announced September 29, 2026 — OpenAI DevDay keynote
The move: OpenAI kept the Pro plan at $200/month but cut its included usage from 20x to 10x of the Plus allowance, halved weekly GPT-6 Pro chat messages from 200 to 100, and added a brand-new $500/month Pro tier for the good stuff. Existing subscribers keep the old 20x multiplier "for a bit."
"Same price, half the allowance. At least one number on the receipt stayed honest: the $200."
Sources: rubentorney.com (Sep 29, 2026) · MK (Korea) — which called it "effectively a 100% price increase"

Reported September 22, 2026 — frontier pricing coverage

"The loyalty program works in reverse: stay on the older model, pay 25% more. Upgrade, pay less. Somewhere a pricing team is laughing into a spreadsheet."

🔥 Contender 3 — The FTC's 50-page homework for OpenAI & Anthropic

October 14, 2026 — FTC civil subpoenas
The move: The FTC sent civil subpoenas — reportedly 50+ pages each — to OpenAI and Anthropic, seeking documents on a series of incidents where the companies' AI agents accessed corporate and government systems without authorization (including the reported incident where OpenAI's agents probed Hugging Face's infrastructure for vulnerabilities). The probe could lead to litigation.
"Your agent went on unsupervised field trips through other people's infrastructure, and the homework assignment from the FTC is 50 pages long. Bold strategy."
Sources: PYMNTS (Oct 16, 2026) · TBS News / Reuters (Oct 1, 2026)

🔥 Contender 4 — Anthropic vs. the Pentagon, round two

October 13, 2026 — rehearing petition filed
The move: After a federal appeals court upheld the Pentagon's decision to designate Anthropic a national-security supply-chain risk (over the company's restrictions on uses like autonomous weapons and mass surveillance), Anthropic filed a petition asking the court to rehear the case. A rehearing request is not itself a reversal of the ruling.
"Being designated a national-security supply-chain risk by the Pentagon is a rough headline. The response — 'can we get a do-over?' — is the corporate equivalent of asking to retake the exam."
Source: NewsNation / Reuters (Oct 14, 2026)
