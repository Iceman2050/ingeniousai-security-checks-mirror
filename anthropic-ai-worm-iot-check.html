ANTHROPIC RESEARCH — OCT 2026 — AI SELF-REPLICATION WARNING

Could an AI worm turn your IoT devices into its brain?

Anthropic just showed it's nearly possible: in lab tests, GLM-5.2 propagated Llama 3.1-8B to all 8 Android nodes in 4 of 5 trials — hopping node-to-node over unauthenticated ADB ports, then standing up an inference server on each victim. A 9B-parameter model ran on 8 of 9 hardware types tested; a 26B model ran distributed across fifteen $55 TV boxes. Anthropic's verdict: small models may be months away from self-replicating on commercial IoT — with no centralized point to shut off. Tick the 60-second checklist to see if your fleet is exposed. The 60-second exposure check — tick what applies

Close the ADB hole today

nmap -p 5555 192.168.1.0/24 # find exposed ADB # On each hit: disable ADB / ADB-over-network in # developer settings, or firewall the port
  • ADB over network is a development feature — it hands over the entire device stack. It should never be on in production, on any device, ever.
  • Same treatment for telnet (23), unauthenticated SSH, open UPnP — every unauthenticated management interface is a hop point.

Why defenders should take this seriously — and stay honest

  • What's proven: the full replication chain works — hop via open ADB, stand up inference, serve the weights. The inference-setup step researchers once called the bottleneck no longer blocks it, and it runs without root.
  • What's NOT proven: a small model choosing to replicate on its own. Anthropic orchestrated the worm with GLM-5.2 simulating a future small model. This is a capability demo, not a self-willed worm — and no full rogue replicator exists yet.
  • Not everyone buys the alarm. Some cybersecurity experts share Anthropic's concern; at least one called it “vendor exaggeration,” arguing models are still too big to move around efficiently and can't draw on enough compute to operate independently. Anthropic's own numbers partly concede the point: replication would be slow — a day or more per copy, with even a Kimwolf-scale botnet amounting to roughly 40 Nvidia H100s — which gives defenders room to respond by flagging large transfers, quarantining hosts, and hardening cheap devices.
  • The real near-term risk isn't Skynet — it's criminals using AI agents to automate the existing IoT-compromise playbook at scale, plus model weights becoming just another malware payload.
  • Anthropic's own mitigations: stronger default security, better network monitoring, isolating infected devices.
