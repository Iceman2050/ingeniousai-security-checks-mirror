What "ready" covers — the 4 Article 50 scenarios

50(1) — AI interaction

Users must know they're talking to AI, not a human. Chatbots, voice agents, AI support — disclosed at first interaction.

50(2) — Synthetic content

AI-generated image, audio, video, and certain text must be marked as artificially generated — visibly to people and detectably by machines.

50(3) — Emotion & biometrics

Emotion recognition and biometric categorization systems must inform people they're being analyzed.

50(4) — Deepfakes

AI-generated or manipulated content resembling real persons, places, or events must be disclosed as synthetic.

FAQ

Does the badge make me compliant?

No — it's a signal, not a certification. It tells visitors you've addressed the four scenarios and links them to what that means. Real compliance is the disclosures themselves, kept current.

When did Article 50 become enforceable?

The transparency obligations under Article 50 became applicable on 2 August 2026. Enforcement is active now.

I'm not in the EU — does this apply?

The Act applies to providers and deployers placing AI systems on the EU market, regardless of where they're based. If EU users touch your AI, check your obligations with qualified counsel.

What are the labelling requirements for AI-generated content?

The notice must be clear and distinguishable, and given at first exposure. Providers must mark AI-generated content so it is machine-readable and detectable, with a phase-in deadline of 2 December 2026 for systems already on the market.

How do I label my content as AI-generated?

Put a visible notice on the content itself — not buried in a caption — at first exposure, and add machine-readable provenance such as C2PA Content Credentials or a watermark. Keep a dated record of every labelled asset.
