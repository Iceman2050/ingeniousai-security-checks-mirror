ORIGINAL RESEARCH · OCT 17, 2026

We audited 16 companies that make or serve AI-generated ads. The platforms label AI content visibly. The AI-ad industry doesn't.

Manually reviewed on October 17, 2026 — public pages only, no logins, no scraping beyond what any visitor sees. 8 of 16 show visible AI-disclosure labeling to end viewers. All 8 are platform or tool giants with documented labeling programs. The other 8 — agencies and SaaS vendors whose entire business is producing AI-generated ad creative — show no visible AI label on their own published example ads.

A pattern inside the gap: 5 of the 8 vendors market their output as "authentic UGC" or creator-style ads — formats whose whole selling point is that a viewer cannot tell the presenter isn't a real customer. The labeling obligation the EU AI Act's Article 50 places on deployers lands squarely on the brands running these ads, not the vendors — and neither side is labeling.

What this means with 46 days to the deadline

December 2, 2026 is when the EU AI Act's transparency/marking duties phase in alongside the Digital Omnibus's new Article 5 prohibitions. Our audit found the enforcement surface already labeled on the platforms — Meta, TikTok, YouTube, and Google all run visible, documented AI-labeling programs with detection that can retroactively label undisclosed AI content. The exposure sits downstream: brands and agencies buying synthetic ad creative get no disclosure help from the vendors, and nothing in what we reviewed shows those vendors warning buyers that they carry the deployer disclosure duty under Article 50. That is the compliance gap DisclosureGuard-style tooling exists to close.

Methodology & reproducibility

Sample selection. 16 companies, in two deliberately contrasting halves. Half A: 8 platform/tool providers with documented public AI-labeling programs (Meta, TikTok, YouTube, Google, OpenAI, Adobe, Getty Images, Canva) — chosen because each operates a labeling system a reviewer can check against public policy sources. Half B: 8 AI-ad creative vendors (agencies and SaaS tools producing AI-generated ad creative), selected as representative of the 2026 AI-ad industry from public comparison roundups of AI video/UGC ad tools — anonymized in this report.

Review method. Each subject's public homepage and published example creative/pages were reviewed on October 17, 2026 via normal browsing — no logins, no purchases, no accounts, no scraping beyond the page text any visitor sees. For Half A, findings were corroborated against each company's public policy/help documentation. Fields recorded per subject: sector, AI content observed (yes/no/unclear), visible disclosure label present (yes/no), and a label-quality note.

Naming rule. Brands with positive findings are named and praised; brands without visible labeling stay anonymized ("Agency #1", "Vendor #2", etc.) — this is an audit of a market gap, not a shaming list.

Limitations — read these before citing us. (1) Text-based page review cannot assess visual creative provenance — we judged what the public pages claim and show, not pixels. (2) Half B was assessed on vendors' public sites, not on live ads their clients run — client ads may be labeled by the platforms that serve them. (3) Platform policies change fast; TikTok's, Meta's and YouTube's 2026 labeling rules have all been revised mid-year. (4) "Visible label" was judged from a desktop review; label placement can differ by device and app version. (5) Canva counts as "terms-only": its disclosure duty sits contractually on the user, which is materially weaker than platform-applied labels.

Reproducible from public pages: every positive finding above links to the policy it rests on — search any brand name plus "AI label policy 2026".
