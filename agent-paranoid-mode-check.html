VITALIK'S PARANOID MODE — OCT 16, 2026

Vitalik built "paranoid mode" for AI agents. How exposed is yours?

On October 16, 2026, Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin announced on X that he had built an experimental "paranoid mode" wrapper for his AI agent harness, pi: an OpenAI-compatible daemon layering Tor (a separate circuit per conversation), zkAPI zero-knowledge payments, random cover traffic, and local LLM prompt rewriting — with the harness itself sandboxed (bwrap) and wrapped in torsocks so everything it does goes through Tor. His goal: stop agents leaking identity along the three axes every agent exposes — what you request, how you pay for it, and where you're making the request from. The code is open-source. And he was blunt about the limits (below). This 60-second check measures your agent's exposure on those same three axes. Free, no signup.

What Vitalik built (verified, Oct 16, 2026):
• Tor, per conversation: in his words, "each conversation gets assigned a separate Tor circuit" — so no two tasks share a network identity.
• Cover traffic: requests are masked with random cover traffic, hiding timing and frequency patterns from passive observers.
• zkAPI payments: zero-knowledge payments (private ETH deposits via Open Anonymity / Ethereum Foundation) so API billing doesn't link queries to an identity.
• Local prompt rewriting: a local model (he's using qwen3.8-flash-next) rewrites prompts before they reach the external provider — stripping identifying details and stylistic fingerprints.
• Sandboxed harness: the pi agent harness runs in a bwrap sandbox (MicroVM experiments ongoing), wrapped with torsocks — all its network activity forced through Tor.
• His own caveats: real latency (especially starting new conversations); cover traffic is weak if the LLM provider colludes with a Tor guard and manipulates timing; Tor is "far from optimal" — he argues for a specialized mixnet with a higher bandwidth multiplier and built-in constant cover traffic.

Why agent privacy matters — the three axes

  • What you request. Prompts are the richest data an AI provider holds on you: project names, company internals, negotiation positions, medical or legal details, and your stylistic fingerprint. A local rewriting pass strips this before it leaves your machine.
  • How you pay. An API key or card links every single query to a billing identity — one subpoena, one breach, and the whole history is deanonymized. Zero-knowledge payment protocols like zkAPI break that link with cryptographic proofs instead of trust.
  • Where you request from. IP addresses, TLS fingerprints, and request timing let observers cluster your sessions even without content. Separate Tor circuits per conversation plus cover traffic make that clustering expensive — though, as Vitalik himself notes, not impossible for a determined, colluding adversary.
  • The uncomfortable truth. Most agents today leak on all three axes simultaneously: your personal IP, your named API account, your raw prompts. "Paranoid mode" is what it looks like when someone closes all three at once — and the latency bill shows why almost nobody does.
