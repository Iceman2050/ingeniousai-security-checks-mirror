Cybersecurity News Today: The CISA KEV Deadline for BeyondTrust and Fortinet Has Passed

The loudest deadline in this week's cybersecurity news today cycle expired yesterday. On October 16, 2026, CISA added two vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog and gave federal agencies just three days — until October 19, 2026 — to remediate them under Binding Operational Directive 26-04. The pair: CVE-2026-102764, an authentication bypass in BeyondTrust Remote Support, and CVE-2025-61624, a path traversal flaw across Fortinet's FortiOS, FortiPAM, FortiProxy, and FortiSwitchManager. Both are in the catalog for one reason: there is evidence they are being actively exploited. The deadline has passed. The exposure has not.

Honest scope — read this first: KEV due dates under BOD 26-04 are legally binding only on US federal civilian agencies. If you are a private company, nobody fines you for missing October 19. But do not mistake "not legally binding on me" for "not urgent for me": a KEV listing means CISA has evidence of exploitation in the wild. The deadline is the government's clock. The risk is yours.

The two vulnerabilities, exactly as CISA lists them

  • CVE-2026-102764 — BeyondTrust Remote Support. Vulnerability name: "Authentication Bypass by Spoofing" (CWE-290). CISA's description: the flaw "could allow a remote attacker to perform file operations on the appliance." Vendor advisory: BT26-05. Added to KEV 2026-10-16, due 2026-10-19.
  • CVE-2025-61624 — Fortinet multiple products. Vulnerability name: "Path Traversal" (CWE-22), affecting FortiOS, FortiPAM, FortiProxy, and FortiSwitchManager. CISA's description: it "may allow an authenticated attacker with admin profile and at least read-write permissions to write or delete arbitrary files via specific CLI commands." Vendor advisory: FG-IR-26-122. Added to KEV 2026-10-16, due 2026-10-19.

For both entries, CISA's required action is the same formula: apply mitigations in accordance with the vendor's instructions, comply with BOD 26-04 and CISA's forensics triage requirements, and — if mitigations are unavailable — discontinue use of the product. Why this cybersecurity news today matters: a three-day window

KEV due dates are risk-tiered: CISA gives agencies longer for lower-risk flaws. A three-day turnaround over a weekend is close to the shortest fuse the catalog uses, and it tells you two things. First, exploitation is not theoretical — KEV admission requires evidence of active exploitation, and independent reporting has tied the BeyondTrust flaw to an active intrusion campaign (see our BeyondTrust zero-day exposure check for the campaign detail). Second, the affected products sit in the worst possible place: remote support appliances and network edge devices — systems that are internet-facing by design and hold privileged access by function. An authentication bypass on a remote support appliance is not a bug in a brochure site; it is a door into the tool your admins use to reach everything else.

The Fortinet flaw is old. That is exactly why it is on this list.

CVE-2025-61624 carries a 2025 identifier and Fortinet published its fix months ago, in advisory FG-IR-26-122. So why did CISA list it in October 2026 with a three-day fuse? Because KEV listing tracks exploitation, not disclosure. A patch existing and a patch being applied are different things, and edge appliances are famously left unpatched for months. The catalog entry is CISA saying, in effect: attackers are using this now, against the organizations that never got around to the fix. If your FortiOS, FortiPAM, FortiProxy, or FortiSwitchManager is still unpatched, the age of the fix is not reassurance — it is the problem.

Missed the deadline? Do this now

  • Inventory first: find every BeyondTrust Remote Support appliance and every Fortinet FortiOS / FortiPAM / FortiProxy / FortiSwitchManager instance you run, including the ones "nobody owns anymore."
  • Apply the vendor mitigations: BT26-05 for BeyondTrust, FG-IR-26-122 for Fortinet. If a mitigation genuinely does not exist for your version, CISA's guidance is to discontinue use until it does.
  • Run forensics triage: CISA's required action explicitly includes its Forensics Triage Requirements — patching a box that is already compromised just gives the intruder a freshly patched hiding place.
  • Check internet exposure: CISA's required action makes stakeholders responsible for evaluating each asset's internet exposure. Edge and remote-support systems should never be more exposed than the job demands.
  • Private sector: treat the passed deadline as your internal deadline too. KEV is the closest thing to a public, evidence-based patch-priority list that exists.

Frequently asked questions

What was the October 19, 2026 CISA KEV deadline?

It was the remediation due date CISA set under Binding Operational Directive 26-04 for two vulnerabilities added to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog on October 16, 2026: CVE-2026-102764 in BeyondTrust Remote Support and CVE-2025-61624 in Fortinet products. Federal civilian agencies were required to apply vendor mitigations by October 19, 2026 — a three-day window that signals how serious CISA considers both flaws.

Which products do the two CVEs affect?

CVE-2026-102764 affects BeyondTrust Remote Support (see vendor advisory BT26-05). CVE-2025-61624 affects Fortinet FortiOS, FortiPAM, FortiProxy, and FortiSwitchManager (see Fortinet advisory FG-IR-26-122).

Does the KEV deadline legally apply to private companies?

No. KEV due dates under BOD 26-04 are legally binding only on US federal civilian executive branch agencies. For private organizations the deadline is not a legal obligation — but it is CISA's strongest public prioritization signal, because a vulnerability only enters the KEV catalog when there is evidence of active exploitation. If you run these products unpatched, the deadline passing does not reduce your risk; the exploitation is the risk.

What is CVE-2026-102764 in BeyondTrust Remote Support?

It is an authentication bypass by spoofing vulnerability (CWE-290). Per CISA's catalog description, it could allow a remote attacker to perform file operations on the appliance. CISA's required action is to apply mitigations per the vendor's instructions (advisory BT26-05), follow BOD 26-04 and forensics triage requirements, and discontinue use of the product if mitigations are unavailable.

What should you do if you missed the deadline?

Patch or apply the vendor mitigations now — the deadline passing does not close the exposure. Then run CISA's forensics triage requirements to check for signs of compromise, evaluate each affected asset's internet exposure, and, if mitigations are genuinely unavailable, follow CISA's guidance to discontinue use of the product until they are.
