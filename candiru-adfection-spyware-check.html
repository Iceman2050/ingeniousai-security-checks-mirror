ADFECTION — ZERO-CLICK SPYWARE VIA ADS — CONFIRMED OCT 15, 2026

Spyware is now delivered through ads you never click

On October 15, 2026, the University of Toronto's Citizen Lab published the first public technical proof that mercenary spyware reached real devices through targeted online ads. The ad firm Discover-Tech — a Google-certified vendor — used its Controlium platform to fingerprint devices in ad auctions and serve chosen targets an ad that silently installed Candiru spyware the instant it rendered. At least 45 devices at Al Jazeera were infected in 2024–2025. Nobody clicked anything. Then, the same day, Reuters reported that a second investigation by ad-security firm Confiant showed the operation was vastly bigger than the infection story alone: 10 million ads a day, a tracking network spanning 120,000 websites and 75,000 apps, phone numbers extracted from smartphones, and cookie matching that tracked people even beyond the reach of the ads. "Very likely hundreds of millions of people would have been touched," Confiant said. This 60-second check tells you your risk level and exactly how to shrink it. Free, no signup.

Update Oct 16 — the operation was bigger than first reported

  • 10 million ads a day pumped out worldwide by Discover-Tech, per Confiant — ordinary product ads for phones, earpods, and more, running through its various ad-platform partnerships.
  • 120,000+ websites and 75,000 apps carried the ads. The ad only needed to load on a page for the spying to occur — no click, no consent, no warning.
  • Phone-number extraction: the operation sometimes pulled phone numbers from smartphone users — linking your device to your real identity for follow-on targeting.
  • Cookie matching tracked people beyond the ads' reach: data-sharing arrangements with ad partners let Discover-Tech follow people across the web even where its ads never appeared.
  • Customers included state-level spyware firms: besides Candiru (blacklisted by the US government), other Israeli surveillance firms got access through Discover-Tech to ad platforms run by Google, Comcast, and Russia's Yandex.
  • Who the ads reached (new, via FT/Reuters): thousands of Discover-Tech ads were served inside networks tied to NATO, the UN, the European Commission, 12+ national governments, the Pentagon, the IAEA, the ICC — and chipmakers Nvidia, Sandisk, Broadcom, Micron, and Arm. Served there does not mean compromised there — but it maps exactly who this pipeline was aimed at: officials, military staff, and semiconductor engineers.
  • "The way online advertising works today is a systemic security threat," Citizen Lab senior researcher Wolfie Cristl told Reuters — for states, the private sector, civil society, and everyone else alike.
Not just a targeted-spyware story anymore: the targeting pipeline (real-time bidding data, ad IDs, phone numbers) touches everyone who sees ads. The hygiene in steps 2–3 below starves that pipeline.

The 60-second risk check

1

Check whether you're in the targeted profile — mercenary spyware is sold to governments for targeted surveillance. The confirmed victims were journalists and media staff. Ask yourself honestly:

  • Are you a journalist, activist, lawyer, politician, or government staffer?
  • Do you work on stories or cases a government might want to surveil?
Yes to either → you are in the targetable population. Do step 2 and 3 today — not this weekend.
No → your direct risk is low, but the delivery channel touches everyone. Discover-Tech ads ran on ~200,000 websites and apps. The hygiene in steps 2–3 protects you against the whole class, not just this vendor.
2

Check your device hardening — zero-click ad attacks exploit the browser and OS rendering the ad. Hardened devices break the chain:

  • iPhone: Settings → Privacy & Security → Lockdown Mode → Turn On. This is Apple's built-in extreme-protection mode, designed exactly for mercenary-spyware targets.
  • Android: enable Advanced Protection (Settings → Security & privacy) and keep Google Play Protect on.
  • Both: install every pending OS and browser update now — spyware chains burn on unpatched rendering bugs.
  • Both: reset your advertising ID (iOS: Settings → Privacy & Security → Apple Advertising; Android: Settings → Privacy → Ads → Reset advertising ID) and enable "limit ad tracking" — this is the identifier the targeting pipeline keys on.
Lockdown Mode / Advanced Protection ON + fully updated + ad ID reset → best available posture against this attack class.
3

Check your network-level blocking — Citizen Lab and Confiant published indicators, including the ad-server domain mtrtb[.]com:

  • If you run Pi-hole, NextDNS, or a DNS filter: block mtrtb.com and watch Citizen Lab's report for additional IOCs.
  • Consider a tracker-blocking DNS (e.g. a filtered NextDNS profile) on your phone full-time — it neuters the ad-auction data collection this technique feeds on.
  • Use a content blocker in your mobile browser (e.g. 1Blocker on iOS, uBlock Origin on Android Firefox) — fewer ad slots rendered, fewer chances.
High-risk individuals (journalists, dissidents): contact Citizen Lab or a digital-security helpline like Access Now for a device check — don't rely on self-assessment alone.

Why this changes the threat model

  • The trusted-vendor badge was the cover. Discover-Tech sat on Google's certified-vendor lists and IAB Europe's approved list from 2020 until October 2026 — six years. Certification screened nobody.
  • Real-time bidding is a surveillance firehose. Every ad auction broadcasts device, location, and behavioral data to hundreds of bidders. Citizen Lab analyzed 4 billion Discover-Tech bidding records; the same data any bidder sees was used to pick targets.
  • Scale is the scary part. Beyond the 45 confirmed Al Jazeera infections, millions of devices were covertly directed to contact Candiru-linked servers — likely large-scale tracking and reconnaissance.
  • It's not over. Citizen Lab says a separate actor, DARK CARTOGRAPHER, is using the same infrastructure to exploit mobile-carrier vulnerabilities and link advertising IDs to phone numbers — mass de-anonymization. Full report forthcoming.

What to do right now

  • Update everything — phone OS, browsers, apps. Unpatched rendering engines are what zero-click chains burn on.
  • Turn on Lockdown Mode (iOS) or Advanced Protection (Android) if you're in any targeted profession.
  • Reset your advertising ID and limit ad tracking — starve the targeting pipeline of its key identifier.
  • Block the published IOCs at DNS level (mtrtb.com and any additions in Citizen Lab's report).
  • Don't trust vendor badges alone — "certified ad vendor" meant nothing here. Defense is device hardening, not supply-chain trust.
