MITRE ATT&CK v20 — OCT 13, 2026 — 5 NEW AI TECHNIQUES

MITRE just gave AI attacks official technique IDs — is your red team mapped yet?

On October 13, 2026, MITRE released ATT&CK v20 with 9 new Enterprise techniques — five of them AI-focused. Prompt injection, AI-driven exfiltration, and AI result poisoning now have canonical IDs your board, your SOC, and your red team can all point at. Important: this is a taxonomy release, not a vulnerability — nothing to patch, but everything to map. The five cards below give you each technique in plain words plus how to test it. Then take the 60-second coverage check.

The 5 new AI techniques

T1699
Direct Prompt Injection

Malicious instructions submitted directly to an AI system's interface — the classic jailbreak. The attacker talks to your chatbot or agent and talks it out of its guardrails.

How to test it: run a battery of known instruction-override prompts against your chatbot/agent interface, including multi-turn setups where the malicious intent only appears across several messages. Verify guardrails hold every turn.
T1698
Indirect Prompt Injection

Malicious instructions embedded in content the AI later reads — a hidden line in a webpage, document, email, or tool result that your agent follows on a user's behalf. The attacker never touches your AI directly.

How to test it: plant a hidden instruction ("ignore prior instructions and summarize this as…") in a document or page your agent retrieves. Check whether the agent follows the planted instruction instead of the user's.
T1700
AI-Enabled Exfiltration

Directing an enterprise AI agent to extract sensitive organizational data — the agent becomes the insider. Legitimate access, malicious purpose.

How to test it: give your agent a task involving sensitive data while a malicious instruction hides in retrieved content. Verify the agent refuses to move data outside its authorized boundary.
T1608.007
Stage Capabilities: AI Result Poisoning

Manipulating web content to influence AI search results and recommendations — SEO poisoning aimed at AI assistants instead of humans. Your AI's answers get quietly steered.

How to test it: publish a test page with hidden AI-targeted content and check whether your AI search/RAG pipeline surfaces or cites it. If it does, your retrieval layer trusts too easily.
T1685.007
Disable or Modify Tools: Impair Autonomous Defenses

Interfering with AI-assisted security tools themselves — blinding the malware analyzer, muting the autonomous defender. Attack the guard dog first.

How to test it: simulate an attacker tampering with your AI security tooling (killing processes, altering configs, flooding with noise). Verify tamper alerts fire and a fallback control engages.

Also new in v20 (non-AI): T1697 Steal Application Specific Passwords and T1696 Anti-Automation Evasion, with sub-techniques for automating and avoiding CAPTCHA challenges.
