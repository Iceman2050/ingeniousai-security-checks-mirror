Frequently asked questions — FAQ

What is Google's Enhanced protection mode in Messages?

An opt-in setting announced October 14, 2026 for Google Messages. It lets Scam Detection use cloud Gemini models inside Private AI Compute — where data is encrypted and processed in a secure environment Google says even it cannot access — to evaluate complex message context and a scammer's intent in conversations with unknown senders. It is rolling out over the coming weeks to Pixel 6 and newer, Galaxy S24 series and newer, and other AICore devices.

How do I protect my Android phone from scam calls?

Layer the protections: enable Enhanced protection in Google Messages, keep link warnings on, let Android 15+ mask login and payment fields during screen shares, make sure call-forwarding protection is active (Pixel and select devices from early December 2026), and keep your bank's official app installed so verified financial calls can authenticate real bank callers. Never share one-time codes or your screen with an unverified caller.

How does Android verify real bank calls?

Verified financial calls work with participating banking apps to check in real time whether an incoming call was genuinely initiated by the bank. If a caller claiming to represent a bank fails verification, Android ends the call and alerts the user. Per Google, the feature has blocked more than 3 million spoofed calls impersonating Banco do Brasil and Itaú Unibanco since launching in June 2026.

How can I block scams on Android using screen-share protection?

Rolling out this month on Android 15 and newer, automatic screen-share privacy blanks login and payment fields in Chrome and Android WebView during live screen shares — so scammers who pressure you into sharing your screen cannot see your passwords, banking details, or card numbers. Verify it by sharing your screen with Chrome open and confirming sensitive fields are masked.
