OPENAI vs APPLE — TRADE SECRET CASE — OCT 2026

An employee allegedly walked out with 100+ secret files. Would your offboarding catch it?

In a court filing on October 14, 2026, OpenAI accused an unnamed former employee — who had previously worked at Apple, spent six months at OpenAI, then went back to Apple — of sending more than 100 highly confidential files about at least one unreleased OpenAI device to personal accounts. The files allegedly included detailed images of the device and highly confidential engineering and manufacturing data. OpenAI says the last transfer happened on June 2, 2026 — the employee's final day — and that the employee later presented OpenAI's product plans to Apple's senior leadership. Apple says it found no evidence supporting the claims and will cooperate on remediation. None of this is proven — it is an allegation in an escalating trade-secret war (Apple sued OpenAI first, in July). But the alleged method is textbook: bulk exfiltration to personal accounts on the way out the door. This 60-second data breach check tests whether your offboarding would catch it. Free, no signup.

• Employee works at Apple, joins OpenAI for six months, then returns to Apple.

• The last alleged transfer: June 2, 2026 — the employee's final day.
• The employee allegedly presents OpenAI's product plans to Apple's senior leadership, including Apple's VP of operations in Asia.
• OpenAI points to Apple's new push into AI-enabled home products this week as the payoff.
• Apple's response: no evidence found; strict policies against using prior employers' secrets; will help remediate anything found.

Both sides, in one place

  • OpenAI alleges: a former employee moved 100+ confidential files about an unreleased device to personal accounts, then presented OpenAI's product plans to Apple's senior leadership — and Apple's new AI home-device push is the payoff. Filed October 14, 2026, in federal court in San Jose.
  • Apple says: no evidence supports the allegations; strict policies bar using prior employers' secrets; it will cooperate to remediate any OpenAI information found on its systems. Its lawyers note the personal numbers cited don't match the employee's Apple work number.
  • The wider war: Apple sued OpenAI first, in July 2026, accusing two former employees of taking Apple trade secrets — including hardware files — to OpenAI's hardware division. OpenAI denies that too. Two giants, two sets of unproven allegations, one escalating fight over AI hardware talent.

Why this matters beyond the courtroom

Whether or not OpenAI's allegations hold up, the alleged method — bulk transfers to personal accounts during the final days of employment — is a textbook insider-threat pattern. The controls that would catch it (same-day deprovisioning, DLP on personal-account uploads, forensic access logs, structured offboarding) are the same controls every company with trade secrets needs. Lawsuits make headlines; missing offboarding checklists make victims.
