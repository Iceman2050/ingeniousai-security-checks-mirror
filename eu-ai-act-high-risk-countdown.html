When do the high-risk rules apply?

Direct answer: High-risk AI systems under the EU AI Act must meet Chapter III duties — risk management, data governance, technical documentation, human oversight, and more. Standalone Annex III systems: December 2, 2027. AI embedded in regulated products (Annex I): August 2, 2028. Prohibited practices already carry fines up to €35M or 7% of turnover.

AI Agents FAQ

When do the EU AI Act's high-risk obligations apply?

Standalone high-risk systems (Annex III) must comply from December 2, 2027. AI embedded in regulated products as safety components (Annex I) must comply from August 2, 2028. These fixed dates were set by the Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, which entered into force on July 27, 2026.

What are the fines for breaching the EU AI Act?

Three tiers: prohibited practices (Article 5) carry up to 35 million euros or 7% of worldwide annual turnover under Article 99(3); most other breaches, including high-risk obligations, carry up to 15 million euros or 3% under Article 99(4). General-purpose AI model obligations enforced by the Commission run up to 15 million euros or 3% of turnover.

What counts as a high-risk AI system?

Two routes: systems listed in Annex III (use cases like recruitment, credit scoring, biometric identification, education, law enforcement, and public benefits) and AI embedded as safety components in products already regulated under Annex I EU laws (machinery, medical devices, vehicles, and similar). The obligation mapper on this page covers the common categories.

Do the duties apply to deployers or only providers?

Both roles have duties. Providers carry the Chapter III requirements (risk management, documentation, conformity assessment, and more). Deployers must ensure human oversight, monitor the system in use, inform affected persons where required, and keep logs. If you substantially modify a system or put your name on it, you can become the provider.
