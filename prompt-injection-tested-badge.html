What is indirect prompt injection?

Indirect prompt injection hides malicious instructions in content the model reads — a webpage, document, email, or tool output — rather than in the user's own message. It accounts for over 55% of observed prompt-injection attacks in 2026 and succeeds more often because the instructions arrive via trusted sources.

What is the most effective defense against prompt injection?

Deterministic controls, not filters: least-privilege tool permissions, human approval for irreversible actions, strict output handling, and isolation of untrusted content. Filters are probabilistic and attackers routinely bypass them — containment limits what a hijacked model can actually do.
