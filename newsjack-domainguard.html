PHISHING STARTS WITH A LOOKALIKE DOMAIN

Is someone typosquatting your brand right now?

Phishing campaigns start with a typosquatting domain that looks like yours: one letter swapped, a hyphen added, a lookalike character. Every one of those domains leaves a public trace in certificate transparency logs. Search those logs for your brand in 30 seconds. No signup, nothing installed.

The 30-second brand check

1

Search certificate transparency logs. Open crt.sh and search for certificates containing your brand name:

https://crt.sh/?q=%25yourbrand%25 (replace "yourbrand" with your brand or domain name, no spaces)

Scan the results for anything you don't recognize — especially domains with swapped letters, extra hyphens, or odd TLDs.

Unfamiliar lookalike domains with recent certificates → INVESTIGATE. Visit carefully (don't log in to anything) or check the domain's age and registrar.
2

Check the classic typos by hand. The most-abused tricks, using example brand "acme":

acme.com → acme-login.com, acme-secure.com (hyphen tricks) acme.com → acrne.com, acem.com (letter swaps / drops) acme.com → acme.net, acme.io, acme.co (TLD swaps) acme.com → аcme.com (lookalike Unicode chars)

Search crt.sh for each variant the same way as step 1.

Any variant registered recently that isn't yours → assume hostile until proven otherwise. Phishers register days before the campaign.
Nothing suspicious → clean today. Squatters register new domains constantly — this check needs repeating.

crt.sh is a free public log operated for the security community. A domain having a certificate doesn't prove malice — but a lookalike you didn't register deserves a look.

If you found a squat, do this now

  • Don't visit it from a work machine — use a sandboxed browser or a URL scanner.
  • Document everything — screenshots, WHOIS, certificate dates. You'll need it for takedown.
  • File abuse reports with the registrar and hosting provider; report phishing URLs to Google Safe Browsing.
  • Warn your customers — a short "we will never ask you to log in at this domain" notice kills most phish.
  • Watch continuously — takedown one domain and the attacker registers three more.

FAQ

What is typosquatting?

Typosquatting is registering domain names that look like a trusted brand — swapped letters, added hyphens, lookalike Unicode characters, or a different TLD — to trick people into visiting a phishing site or trusting a scam email. The 30-second check above hunts for them in certificate transparency logs.

How do I protect my brand from typosquatting?

Register the obvious lookalike variants of your brand yourself, repeat the certificate-transparency search from this page regularly — squatters register new domains constantly — and consider continuous monitoring that alerts you the moment a lookalike is registered.

What is a typosquatted domain?

A typosquatted domain is a lookalike domain registered to exploit typing mistakes or visual similarity with a real brand. Attackers use them for phishing, credential theft, and brand impersonation — every one leaves a public trace in certificate transparency logs.
