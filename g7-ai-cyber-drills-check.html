The G7 is drilling for AI cyber attacks. When did you last drill?

On October 15–16, 2026, G7 finance ministers and central bank governors — meeting on the sidelines of the IMF's annual gathering in Bangkok — agreed to hold tabletop exercises guarding against AI cyber attacks. Japanese Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama announced the plan had been signed off at ministerial level. Simulations designed by G7 finance authorities will test how governments respond to AI-driven attacks — and may pull in private financial institutions. The results will be used to revise response manuals. The trigger: the IMF warned this week that confidence could be undermined by an AI-enabled cyber attack on a critical service of a financial institution, and its stability report named AI-empowered cyber crime among its three biggest perceived threats. IMF data: in 2026, in over 70% of cases, exploitation happened before or on the day of disclosure — up from ~20% in 2018. The Bank of England says defences can no longer be assumed immune to AI-enabled attacks. If the world's finance ministries are rehearsing, your organisation should be too. Why AI attacks change the drill

  • Speed kills playbooks. With over 70% of 2026 exploitation happening before or on disclosure day, the old "patch Tuesday" rhythm is dead — your drill must assume zero warning.
  • AI finds flaws faster than you fix them. The UK's FCA warned frontier models discover vulnerabilities faster than firms remediate — Bank of America just hired 1,000 contractors to patch AI-exposed flaws. Your scenarios must include "the attackers' AI found it first."
  • Confidence is the target, not just systems. The IMF's warning wasn't about downtime — it was about confidence. A hit on one critical financial service can shake the whole sector. Your drill needs a communications track, not just a technical one.
  • Recovery is now a first-class control. The Bank of England is working with banks on deep cyber-recovery options precisely because prevention can no longer be assumed. Untested backups are not backups.
  • Drills that don't rewrite the manual are theatre. The entire point of the G7 exercises is to test protocols and revise them. If your last tabletop changed nothing, it didn't happen.
