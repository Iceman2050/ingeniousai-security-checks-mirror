TECH SUPPORT SCAM WAVE — 20,000+ FAKE ALERT DOMAINS

Is that "Windows Security Alert" real — or a scam?

On October 15, 2026, researchers at Infoblox reported that a long-running scam network called Help TDS — now linked to an affiliate program named Hotejka — is serving fake Microsoft Windows security alerts across more than 20,000 domains on Cloudflare's free hosting. The alerts lock your browser, show a phone number, and the call center on the other end talks victims into installing remote-access software. Spot one in 30 seconds with the check below. No signup, nothing installed.

The 30-second spot-the-scam check

1

You can't close the tab. The page goes fullscreen, or the alert pops straight back when you try to leave. Real Microsoft warnings never trap your browser.

2

It shows a phone number to call. Microsoft will never ask you to call a number from a browser popup. That number reaches a scam call center, not support.

3

Scare language and fake error codes. "Virus detected!", "Your computer is blocked", long codes like 0x800... — all written to panic you into acting fast.

4

It tells you to install something. Especially "remote support" or screen-sharing software. That is the actual goal: getting inside your computer.

5

Check the address bar. If it is not microsoft.com, it is not Microsoft. This wave runs on throwaway domains, many on free pages.dev hosting.

Two or more of these → it is a scam. Close it, call nobody, install nothing.
A genuine Windows Security notification comes from the Windows Security app on your own PC — never from a website, never with a phone number.

A fake alert just popped up — do this

  • Do not call the number. Do not install anything. Nothing on that page can help you; everything on it can hurt you.
  • Kill the tab: press Alt+F4 on Windows (or force-quit the browser on Mac). If the browser offers to restore tabs, say no.
  • Revoke notification permission if you ever clicked "Allow" on a suspicious popup: Settings → Privacy → Site settings → Notifications — remove the site. This is how the follow-up spam reaches you.
  • Warn the people scammers want most: older relatives and anyone who panics at a virus warning. Send them this page.

Why this wave is hard to stop

  • It runs like a business: hacked websites funnel visitors into a traffic system that profiles each visitor and routes the most convincing targets to the fake alert.
  • Everyone else's traffic gets resold to fake "verify you are human" pages that ask for notification permission — then monetized with spam pushes.
  • The domains are disposable (20,000+ and rotating daily). The scam kit behind them is not — which is why defenders now fingerprint the kit, not the domains.

Source: Infoblox threat research on Help TDS / Hotejka, reported October 15, 2026.
